# Zypact Canonical Decisions

- **Status:** Canonical decision register
- **Owner:** Founder/CEO, with methodology and technical review where required
- **Last updated:** 2026-08-19

This register records the decisions that govern the documentation reset. A later decision may supersede an earlier one only when it is added here with a date, rationale, and affected documents.

## Decision format

Each entry records:

- **Status:** approved, provisional, superseded, or requires research;
- **Decision:** the rule now in force;
- **Reason:** why it was chosen;
- **Effect:** documents, product behavior, or commercial language affected;
- **Revisit trigger:** evidence that requires a new decision.

## D-001 — External measurement cannot use a fixed three-run default

- **Date:** 2026-08-16
- **Status:** Approved
- **Decision:** Three provider runs may be used for an engineering smoke test or explicitly labeled demonstration, but not as a stable external client measurement or visibility percentage.
- **Reason:** A private same-question live test produced absent, listed, and primary observations across three runs, demonstrating that the sample was unstable. The confidential fixture remains outside source control.
- **Effect:** Remove fixed three-run client scoring, `won/partial/lost` aggregation, and three-run measurement claims from active product, sales, report, and engineering documentation.
- **Revisit trigger:** None for the prohibition. A future methodology may define bounded sampling procedures, but it cannot relabel three observations as a stable market measurement without new evidence.

## D-002 — Sampling is a protocol parameter, not a magic number

- **Date:** 2026-08-16
- **Status:** Approved
- **Decision:** Planned baseline and retest runs are versioned protocol parameters. The methodology must consider baseline rate, variance, minimum useful change, uncertainty, budget, and the unit of analysis.
- **Reason:** Replacing three runs with another universal number would repeat the same methodological error.
- **Effect:** Engineering must support configurable sampling without silently falling back to three.
- **Revisit trigger:** Validated pilot data may support protocol templates by category or use case.

## D-003 — Every protocol has technical hard caps and spend controls

- **Date:** 2026-08-16
- **Status:** Approved
- **Decision:** Every project/protocol must be bounded by operator-configured run, spend, time-window, rate, and concurrency limits. Each paid attempt is admitted and reserved before the provider call.
- **Reason:** Configurable scientific sampling cannot create unbounded technical or financial risk.
- **Effect:** V1 scope and engineering acceptance tests include boundary rejection, reservation ordering, retry idempotency, and spend reconciliation.
- **Revisit trigger:** Pilot operating data may change production limit values without changing the invariant.

## D-004 — Perplexity Sonar is the initial directional V1 surface

- **Date:** 2026-08-16
- **Status:** Approved
- **Decision:** V1 uses Perplexity Sonar because the live path currently exists and is auditable and economical. Its results are labeled as directional observations of that recorded surface and configuration.
- **Reason:** API output from other providers may not represent their public consumer or tenant-specific buying experience. Adding provider breadth before validity would increase scope without improving truth.
- **Effect:** No V1 report may present Perplexity results as ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, or “all AI.”
- **Revisit trigger:** A second surface is added only after its access method, public relevance, provenance, cost, and comparability are documented.

## D-005 — V1 is a diagnostic plus registered retest

- **Reason:** “AI Buyer Journey Intelligence” is too broad for the current product. “Verified lift experiment” promises more than the method has yet demonstrated.
- **Effect:** V1 centers on approved buyer questions, recorded protocol, baseline evidence, one intervention hypothesis, an observation window, retest, and Outcome Card.

## D-006 — No causal or revenue guarantee

- **Date:** 2026-08-16
- **Status:** Approved
- **Decision:** Zypact reports observed changes under recorded conditions. It does not claim that an intervention caused a change or that AI exclusion caused a lost sale unless a future design and evidence justify a narrowly stated claim.
- **Reason:** Before/after movement, stable controls, citations, and referral data can still be confounded.
- **Effect:** Every Outcome Card includes a non-removable footer: “This Outcome Card documents an observed change under the recorded conditions. It does not establish that the intervention caused the change.”
- **Revisit trigger:** No sales or UI toggle can override this. A future causal methodology requires independent statistical and legal review plus a new decision.

## D-007 — Causality is not an outcome enum

- **Date:** 2026-08-16

- **Decision:** Outcome status is limited to `increase_observed`, `decrease_observed`, `no_clear_change`, or `inconclusive`. `causality_not_established` is not a selectable result.

- **Effect:** The renderer supplies the standard methodology footer on every Outcome Card.

## D-014 — Canonical observation vocabulary replaces won, partial, lost; mixed results are never collapsed

- **Date:** 2026-08-19
- **Status:** Approved
- **Approval date:** 2026-08-19
- **Decision:** Run-level results use `not_observed_in_run`, `listed_in_run`, `primary_in_run`, `ambiguous_in_run`, and `analysis_failed` in place of `won`/`partial`/`lost`/`not_relevant`. The former `negative` position becomes `listed_in_run` combined with a separate `negative_claim_detected` flag. `not_relevant` becomes a separate context field, not a position. `analysis_failed` is a status distinct from `run_position`, excluded from the observed/not-observed denominator. Question-level results use `observed_consistently_in_sample`, `not_observed_consistently_in_sample`, `mixed_in_sample`, and `insufficient_evidence`; `mixed_in_sample` may never be converted to `won`, `partial`, or `lost`. `insufficient_evidence` is mandatory when completed runs equal zero, and any threshold above that floor is a versioned protocol parameter, not an engineering constant. `canonical_entities` and `entity_aliases` are scoped per project, never shared across tenants. Every canonical observation carries a mandatory, non-null `dataset_id` and `dataset_source_type` (`snapshot_request`, `manual_scan`, or `legacy_unresolved`); `snapshot_request_id` remains nullable on the existing `ProbeRun`/`ProbeResult` structures for compatibility. Legacy compatibility is provided by a strictly additive adapter that modifies exactly one existing report-generation file; no other existing consumer is changed. A golden fixture must confirm that the 15 already-stored HockeyStack runs remain bit-identical after backfill.
- **Reason:** The current per-prompt aggregate verdict is computed by a majority-threshold heuristic that forces a single stable label from what may be an unstable, mixed sample — the same methodological failure D-001 already corrected for the fixed three-run default. `METHODOLOGY.md` §9 and §10 already prescribe the correct vocabulary; it was not yet implemented, and the client-facing aggregate report does not yet honor it.
- **Effect:** `METHODOLOGY.md` (version 1.0 to 1.1). Future implementation work would affect `lib/schemas/probe-analysis.schema.ts`, `lib/db/types.ts`, `lib/db/memory-adapter.ts`, `lib/db/supabase-adapter.ts`, `lib/probe-results/report-selection.ts` (additive only), `lib/export/build-mvp-report.ts` (the only modified legacy consumer), and `scripts/mvp-scan.ts`, plus a future additive migration. No code, migration, or consumer file is changed by this decision entry itself.
- **Revisit trigger:** Pilot data on the real frequency of `mixed_in_sample` and `insufficient_evidence`; completion of the M3 consumer migration, which retires the temporary legacy-column clause of this decision.

## D-015 — Append-only backfill write path for probe_results via a scoped SECURITY DEFINER RPC

- **Date:** 2026-08-19
- **Status:** Approved
- **Approval date:** 2026-08-19
- **Decision:** Backfilling `probe_results.dataset_id`, `dataset_source_type`, `run_position`, `negative_claim_detected`, and `observation_schema_version` onto existing rows is performed exclusively through a single `SECURITY DEFINER` RPC, never through a `GRANT UPDATE` to `service_role` on `probe_results`. The RPC locks the target `probe_results` row `FOR UPDATE`, rereads its five target columns under that lock, locks and validates the referenced `dataset_batches` row (must exist, with matching `project_id` and `dataset_source_type`, else raises `errcode 22023`), and then applies exactly one of three mutually exclusive outcomes: if all five columns already match the requested values (compared with `IS NOT DISTINCT FROM`), it returns `ALREADY_APPLIED` without writing; if all five columns are `NULL`, it writes all five columns in one `UPDATE` guarded by a `WHERE` clause repeating the all-`NULL` precondition, and returns `APPLIED`; in every other case — including a `NULL` `dataset_id` alongside another already-populated or differing column — it returns `CONFLICT` without writing, and no preexisting differing value is ever overwritten. A target row that does not exist for the given project returns `NOT_FOUND`. The function always returns exactly one row with an explicit status column (`APPLIED`, `ALREADY_APPLIED`, `NOT_FOUND`, or `CONFLICT`), never an empty set, so `NOT_FOUND` and `CONFLICT` remain distinguishable. The function is pinned with `set search_path = public, pg_temp`, is revoked from `PUBLIC`/`anon`/`authenticated`, and grants `EXECUTE` to `service_role` only; `service_role` never receives `UPDATE`/`DELETE`/`TRUNCATE` on `probe_results` itself, so the append-only invariant from migration `0036` remains intact at the table-privilege level. No `SET` clause and no function parameter can reach `verdict`, `product_verdict`, `client_position`, `raw_response`/`raw_response_body`, any M1 evidence hash, `project_id`, or `snapshot_request_id` — those columns stay structurally unreachable through this RPC.
- **Reason:** Migration `0036` revoked `UPDATE`/`DELETE`/`TRUNCATE` on `probe_results` from `service_role` as a deliberate evidence-append-only invariant. The M2 backfill (D-014) needs to write five new columns onto already-existing rows, which a blanket `GRANT UPDATE` would satisfy but would also reopen general write access to a table whose append-only property is central to the M1 evidence contract. A narrowly scoped RPC with an explicit, race-safe status contract satisfies the backfill need without weakening that invariant, and reuses the `SECURITY DEFINER` RPC pattern already established for the snapshot worker (migration `0031`).
- **Effect:** A future, separately created migration (numbered after `0038`) adds this RPC and its `REVOKE`/`GRANT` statements only — no table, column, or constraint change, no `NOT NULL`, no data written by the migration itself. The M2 tranche-2 backfill script (not yet created) calls this RPC exclusively for `probe_results` writes; `probe_runs`, which was never append-only restricted, may continue to use ordinary `UPDATE`. No file is changed by this decision entry itself.
- **Revisit trigger:** None for the RPC-only mechanism itself. A future decision to grant `service_role` broader `UPDATE` privilege on `probe_results`, or to change the append-only invariant established by migration `0036`, requires its own separate decision entry.

## D-016 — Golden-fixture preservation is bit-identical only for pre-existing columns; M1 columns stay NULL absent historical proof

- **Date:** 2026-08-19
- **Status:** Approved
- **Approval date:** 2026-08-19
- **Decision:** For any historical dataset used as a golden fixture under D-014 (HockeyStack or any other export later designated as such), the "bit-identical" preservation guarantee applies exactly to the columns that already existed on that data before any backfill: the legacy columns (`verdict`, `product_verdict`, `client_position`, `raw_response`) and any other column already populated at original capture time. Columns introduced by M1 (`raw_response_body`, `raw_response_sha256`, `raw_response_body_sha256`, `raw_probe_request_sha256`, `full_request_body_sha256`) and provider/retry metadata (`provider_http_status`, `provider_latency_ms`, `provider_request_id`, `provider_model`, `retry_count`) must remain `NULL` on any row for which no historical proof of the exact original provider response exists — never fabricated, never computed retroactively. No SHA-256 or other hash may be computed after the fact over content that was not itself captured with M1 fidelity, since doing so would misrepresent byte-for-byte fidelity that was never established.
- **Reason:** The export diagnosed during the M2 tranche-2 staging preflight (15 `probe_runs`/`probe_results`, legacy columns present, all M1 evidence/hash/provider-metadata columns absent) is confirmed pre-M1. Retroactively computing a hash over this data would fabricate the appearance of M1-grade evidence fidelity that was never actually captured — a direct violation of the CLAUDE.md invariant that incomplete or inconsistent evidence fails closed into an honest state, and a necessary clarification of D-014's HockeyStack-fixture clause, which implicitly assumed "preserve bit-identically" meant preserving what already existed, not synthesizing new evidence-grade fields.
- **Effect:** Clarifies/amends D-014's golden-fixture clause without altering its substance; establishes a general rule for any pre-M1 historical dataset encountered during M2 backfill design or execution. No file, code, or migration is changed by this decision entry itself. Any future golden-fixture test (including the limitation already documented in `scripts/test-m2-tranche2-writers.ts`) must explicitly assert this NULL-preservation once a concrete pre-M1 dataset is designated as the fixture.
- **Revisit trigger:** None, unless a HockeyStack dataset genuinely captured under M1 (with its evidence columns actually populated) is later located — D-014's original bit-identical hash-preservation clause would then apply to that dataset as originally written, since it would carry real M1 fidelity.

## D-017 — Append-only entity observation ledger, deterministic v1 resolver, and brand-self exclusion

- **Date:** 2026-08-20
- **Status:** Approved
- **Approval date:** 2026-08-20
- **Decision:** Entity resolution and brand-self exclusion are implemented via four tables, two active-head read views, and exactly two `SECURITY DEFINER` RPCs, with no other write path. Schema: `entity_observations` (append-only) records one row per individual raw mention extracted from a `probe_result` — `probe_result_id`/`probe_run_id`/`generated_prompt_id`/`project_id`/`dataset_id`/`dataset_source_type` all FK-locked, directly and via composite FKs to `probe_results` and to its own `resolution_pass_id`, so every one of those fields is transitively pinned to the same tenant, dataset, and pass by Postgres itself; `canonical_entity_id`/`entity_type` are nullable only for `status in ('ambiguous','unresolved')`, and a single CHECK makes both directions of the brand-self rule explicit in one place — `status='excluded_brand_self'` requires `entity_type='client'`, and `status='resolved'` permits only `entity_type in ('competitor','platform')`, structurally forbidding `resolved`+`client` from ever coexisting; `mention_ordinal`/`observation_generation`/`resolver_version` carry positivity CHECKs; the identity/idempotency key is `UNIQUE(resolution_pass_id, mention_ordinal, observation_generation)`. `entity_resolution_passes` (control-plane, not append-only) tracks one resolution attempt per `(probe_result_id, resolver_version)`: `status` is `in_progress`/`completed`/`abandoned`, partial unique indexes guarantee at most one `completed` and at most one `in_progress` pass per that pair, `expected_mention_count` is fixed at pass start and a pass with `expected_mention_count=0` completes immediately (never waits on an observation that will never arrive), `last_activity_at` (not `started_at`) drives staleness detection for resume-vs-abandon decisions so a legitimately slow but still-active pass is never falsely abandoned, and `dataset_source_type` is carried and FK-locked here too, back to `dataset_batches`. `entity_brand_snapshots` (append-only) freezes the exact brand-term inputs (`company_name`, `domain`, mirror-profile aliases, and their normalized forms) used for a given `(project_id, dataset_id, resolver_version)`, created once and never recomputed, so a historical dataset's resolutions remain reproducible even after the project's live profile later changes. `entity_aliases` gains `alias_generation` (default 1, safe on existing rows since Postgres back-fills a constant default without a rewrite) and its uniqueness key changes from `(project_id, entity_type, normalized_text)` to `(project_id, normalized_text, alias_generation)` — dropping `entity_type` from the key is what makes client/competitor exclusivity a structural property rather than a convention, since only one `entity_type` can ever be active for a given normalized text at a time. `canonical_entities` gains a partial unique index enforcing exactly one `entity_type='client'` row per project. Both migration preconditions this schema change depends on are mandatory, executed before the migration applies, and fail closed with no automatic correction if violated: zero pre-existing `entity_aliases` rows may share `(project_id, normalized_text)` under different `entity_type` values (the old key permitted this; the new one cannot), and zero projects may already have more than one `canonical_entities` row with `entity_type='client'`. Active-head views — `entity_aliases_active` (max `alias_generation` per `(project_id, normalized_text)`) and `entity_observations_active` (max `observation_generation` per `(probe_result_id, mention_ordinal)`, scoped to only the highest-`resolver_version` pass that has actually reached `status='completed'`, so an in-progress or abandoned pass can never contribute a partial result) — are the only sanctioned read path for "what does this currently mean"; no consumer queries the base tables directly for that purpose. Normalization is deterministic v1, no LLM call: Unicode NFKC, NFD diacritic stripping, Unicode case-fold, one trailing legal-suffix strip from a fixed list (`inc`, `inc.`, `incorporated`, `llc`, `l.l.c.`, `ltd`, `ltd.`, `limited`, `corp`, `corp.`, `corporation`, `co`, `co.`, `company`, `plc`, `gmbh`, `ag`, `sa`, `s.a.`, `sas`, `sarl`, `s.a.r.l.`, `bv`, `b.v.`, `nv`, `n.v.`, `pty`, `pty ltd`, `oy`, `ab`, `spa`, `s.p.a.`), Unicode punctuation/symbol removal, whitespace collapse, and a narrow domain-shaped branch (strip only a leading `www.`); brand-self matching is exact-string comparison against this same normalizer's output for the project's company name, domain, domain stem, and Mirror Profile aliases — no separate or fuzzy matching logic. `resolve_and_record_entity_observation`'s alias-side comparison against the active head resolves to exactly: no existing head → insert generation 1, `APPLIED`; head identical (regardless of `resolver_version`) → no write, `ALREADY_APPLIED`; higher `resolver_version` with a differing mapping → insert `head_generation+1`, `SUPERSEDED_PRIOR`; same `resolver_version` with a differing mapping → no write, `CONFLICT`; head is `client` and the proposal is `competitor`/`platform` → the function raises unconditionally, at any `resolver_version`, never returning a row — a `client` identity can never be demoted through this ordinary path. `observation_outcome` is `APPLIED`/`ALREADY_APPLIED`/`CONFLICT` only (three values — a correction is always a new pass at a higher `resolver_version`, producing a fresh generation-1 `APPLIED` row, never a same-pass supersession, so `SUPERSEDED_PRIOR` never fires for observations); `alias_outcome` is `APPLIED`/`ALREADY_APPLIED`/`SUPERSEDED_PRIOR`/`CONFLICT`/`NOT_APPLICABLE` (the last exactly when `status in ('ambiguous','unresolved')`). Both RPCs derive `project_id`/`probe_result_id`/`probe_run_id`/`generated_prompt_id`/`dataset_id`/`dataset_source_type`/`resolver_version` internally from the locked pass row and, via its `probe_result_id`, from `probe_results` — none of these are accepted as independently-supplied, independently-verified parameters, removing an entire class of mismatch preconditions. Both RPCs are `SECURITY DEFINER`, `set search_path = public, pg_temp`, owned by the same role that owns every other object these migrations create (in this project, the role that runs `supabase db push` — never `service_role`), revoked from `PUBLIC`/`anon`/`authenticated`, with `EXECUTE` granted to `service_role` only. `service_role` receives **no** `INSERT`/`UPDATE`/`DELETE`/`TRUNCATE` on any of the four tables or two views — only `SELECT` — so the RPCs are structurally the only writers, not merely the intended ones; every relevant object starts with an explicit `REVOKE ALL` from `PUBLIC`/`anon`/`authenticated`/`service_role` before the narrow `SELECT`/`EXECUTE` grants are applied. This includes revoking the `INSERT` grant migration `0038` gave `service_role` on `entity_aliases`; `canonical_entities` keeps its existing direct-`INSERT` grant from `0038` unchanged, because as a bare identity row with no `raw_text`/`normalized_text`/status column, a direct insert into it cannot attach a mention, resolve an alias, or bypass brand-self exclusion or the one-client-per-project constraint — those guarantees live in `entity_aliases`/`entity_observations`, which are RPC-only, and in the constraint itself, which applies regardless of write path.
- **Reason:** `METHODOLOGY.md` §11 and Report Integrity Gates 6–7 (§16) already require brand-self exclusion and complete-or-visibly-uncertain entity normalization as delivery preconditions, but neither is enforceable today: the read-only audit that preceded this decision found no table linking a resolved or unresolved mention back to the `probe_result`/`probe_run`/question that produced it, no idempotency key, no ambiguity/non-resolution state, no correction mechanism compatible with the existing append-only privilege model, and no mechanism preventing the same normalized text from being registered as both the client's own brand and a competitor. Ten rounds of read-only review surfaced and closed, in turn: an overstated claim that a single `UNIQUE` constraint alone could guarantee client/competitor exclusivity; a real risk of `entity_observations_active` mixing rows from a crashed, partial reprocessing pass; brand-term reproducibility breaking once a project's live profile changes; a genuine security contradiction where granting `service_role` direct table privileges would have made the RPCs' business logic bypassable despite being `SECURITY DEFINER`; and two silent migration-breaking preconditions (the `entity_aliases` cross-type collision, the `canonical_entities` multi-client case) that a naïve `ALTER TABLE` would not have caught before failing or, worse, succeeding on the wrong data.
- **Effect:** This decision entry authorizes design only. Separate, later implementation steps — not authorized here and not performed by this entry — are: the literal PL/pgSQL bodies of `start_or_resume_entity_resolution_pass` and `resolve_and_record_entity_observation` (this entry fixes their contract — parameters, preconditions, locking order, concurrency resolution, return semantics — exhaustively; the executable body text is written and tested against a real database separately, exactly as D-015's RPC was); the future migration itself (provisionally numbered after `0040`) containing every `CREATE TABLE`/`ALTER TABLE`/`CREATE VIEW`/`GRANT`/`REVOKE` statement this decision specifies, gated on both migration preconditions above returning zero rows; a historical reprocessing script (parallel to `scripts/backfill-probe-result-dataset-identity.ts`) that resolves entities for `probe_results` that already existed before this tranche, using only already-stored evidence, never a new LLM call; and any future manual-override mechanism for correcting a resolution outside the ordinary deterministic-resolver path, which is explicitly out of scope for both the resolver and this decision and requires its own separate decision entry. Implementation would also retire `createEntityAlias` (`lib/db/types.ts`, `lib/db/supabase-adapter.ts`, `lib/db/memory-adapter.ts`) once `entity_aliases` becomes RPC-only-written — an audit performed before this decision's approval confirmed its only caller anywhere in the repository is `scripts/test-m2-schema-contract.ts` (the method's own code comment already reads "No caller populates these yet"), so no production code path is affected; that test's direct calls must be rewritten to exercise the same constraint coverage through the new RPC surface, with a regression test asserting the direct-insert call site is gone. No table, column, constraint, RPC, migration, or code file is created or changed by this decision entry itself.
- **Revisit trigger:** Real ambiguous/unresolved rates from pilot use may justify a v2 normalizer or a resolver_version bump to the legal-suffix list; any future introduction of a manual-override correction mechanism, or of LLM-assisted resolution, requires its own separate decision, since this draft explicitly excludes both from v1; the `p_stale_after` default (15 minutes) is an operational parameter, adjustable without a new decision if pilot data shows it is miscalibrated.

## D-018 — Amendment to D-017: RPC resolves the canonical entity internally, enforces brand-self exclusion unconditionally for competitor/platform proposals, and reports pass completion in its return row

- **Date:** 2026-08-20
- **Status:** Approved
- **Approval date:** 2026-08-20
- **Decision:** Amends D-017's `resolve_and_record_entity_observation` contract in three ways, all discovered during pre-implementation audit, none new capability. First: the parameter `p_proposed_canonical_id` is removed — a caller cannot supply an id for an entity that does not yet exist. The RPC resolves the canonical entity internally: for `p_proposed_entity_type = 'client'`, via find-or-create against `canonical_entities(project_id, entity_type='client')`, guarded by `canonical_entities_one_client_per_project`, race-safe via the identical insert-on-conflict-then-reselect pattern already specified for `entity_brand_snapshots`; for `'competitor'`/`'platform'`, by reusing the active alias head's `canonical_entity_id` for `(project_id, normalized_text)` if one exists, or creating a fresh `canonical_entities` row of the proposed type otherwise. Second: before honoring any `'competitor'`/`'platform'` proposal, the RPC unconditionally checks the proposal's `normalized_text` against the current pass's own `brand_snapshot_id`'s `normalized_brand_terms`, regardless of whether a pre-existing alias head exists for that text; a match overrides the proposal to `status = 'excluded_brand_self'`, `entity_type = 'client'`, linked to the project's client entity — closing the gap where a first-ever sighting of the client's own name inside an LLM's `competitorsMentioned` output, independent of `clientMentioned`, would otherwise have nothing to compare against. Third: the function's return row gains `pass_status text`, restricted to exactly `'in_progress'` or `'completed'` — never `'abandoned'`, never null. `'completed'` is returned only by the call that causes the pass's row count to reach `expected_mention_count`; `'in_progress'` is returned when it does not. A call referencing a pass that is already `'abandoned'` or already `'completed'` raises at the existing precondition ("pass must be `in_progress`") before any row is returned — a completed pass is rejected outright by that precondition, so `pass_status` can never be returned for a call made against one; it is reported only by the single call that performs the transition. All other aspects of D-017's contract — locking order, the remaining preconditions, the `observation_outcome`/`alias_outcome` vocabularies, the demote-block precondition protecting an already-established `client` head — are unchanged.
- **Reason:** D-017 as approved implicitly required a caller-supplied canonical entity id for a `resolved`/`excluded_brand_self` proposal, unsatisfiable for a genuinely new entity; its brand-self protection covered only an already-established alias head, leaving a first-sighting LLM-drift case uncovered; and it gave callers no RPC-native completion signal, which pre-implementation review showed is necessary — a mention-processing loop can finish with zero thrown exceptions while the pass never reaches `completed` (a miscounted `expected_mention_count`, a silently-skipped mention, or zero calls happening when they should not have), and only an explicit, verified read of pass status can safely gate whether a caller reports `entityResolutionError`.
- **Effect:** No file, migration, or code is created or changed by this entry itself. It corrects the contract that the still-unwritten migration `0042` and the still-unwritten pipeline wiring (`analyze-probe-response.ts`) must implement — nothing has been built against the prior, incomplete signature.
- **Revisit trigger:** None beyond D-017's own.

## D-019 — Amendment to D-018: v1 normalization is exclusively authoritative in TypeScript; the RPC verifies structural preconditions and brand-term membership only, never a Unicode-equivalence recomputation

- **Date:** 2026-08-20
- **Status:** Approved
- **Approval date:** 2026-08-20
- **Decision:** Retracts the SQL-side `normalize_entity_text_v1` function and the "recompute/verify Unicode-exact equivalence server-side" requirement proposed in the prior, unapproved `0042` planning round (never created, never written to any file). In their place: `lib/evidence/normalize-entity-text.ts` is the sole, authoritative implementation of the v1 normalization algorithm (D-017 §5). Every writer of normalized text — the live pipeline (`analyze-probe-response.ts`), the historical reprocessing script (`scripts/backfill-entity-observations.ts`), and the brand-term computation feeding `entity_brand_snapshots.normalized_brand_terms` — calls this exact module; none may hold or evolve an independent copy of the algorithm. `resolve_and_record_entity_observation` keeps `p_normalized_text` as an ordinary input parameter, supplied by the trusted backend, and keeps validating it structurally — non-empty after trim, alongside the existing `p_mention_ordinal >= 0`/`p_raw_text` non-empty/`p_status` four-value/`p_proposed_entity_type` nullability preconditions — but performs **no** server-side re-derivation of `normalized_text` from `raw_text`, and makes no claim of Unicode-equivalence verification. The one check that *does* remain and requires no such recomputation: whenever `p_status = 'excluded_brand_self'`, or whenever the D-018 unconditional override evaluates a `competitor`/`platform` proposal, the RPC checks `p_normalized_text` for exact membership in the pass's `brand_snapshot_id`'s `normalized_brand_terms` array — a comparison of two already-normalized strings, which is exact string equality with zero Unicode ambiguity, unlike deriving `normalized_text` from raw text in the first place. The RPC remains reachable by `service_role` only, and this decision makes explicit, as a documented trust hypothesis, that `service_role` is exclusively the backend's own server-side credential — never exposed to any end-user-facing surface, never a caller this RPC needs to defend against supplying a maliciously fabricated value. No public endpoint may accept or forward a caller-supplied `normalizedText`/`normalized_text` value into the entity-resolution write path; the value is always computed server-side, by the authoritative module, immediately before the RPC call, never accepted as a request-body field.
- **Reason:** The feasibility audit preceding this decision found no way to port NFKC, diacritic-stripping (Unicode category `Mn`), Unicode case-folding, or punctuation/symbol removal (categories `P*`/`S*`) into PL/pgSQL with proven equivalence to the TypeScript implementation — `normalize()` depends on the Postgres build's bundled Unicode Character Database version, `lower()` is a locale-dependent simple case mapping (not true Unicode case-folding, and not what either PostgreSQL's collation system or JavaScript natively provides), and POSIX regex classes (`[[:punct:]]`) are OS/libc-derived approximations with no "symbol" category at all; `unaccent` is a static, hand-curated substitution table, not a live Unicode-category computation. None of this could honestly be declared `IMMUTABLE` either, since it depends on runtime locale/collation and build-time Unicode-table state. Given `resolve_and_record_entity_observation` is reachable only via `service_role` — a credential no end-user-facing code path ever holds — re-deriving Unicode-exact equivalence in SQL was found to defend against a threat that does not exist in this system's actual trust boundary, while introducing a second, harder-to-verify implementation of the same algorithm that could silently drift from the authoritative one. The correct, honest mitigation is a single canonical implementation, used identically everywhere, backed by TypeScript-side tests, plus a structural guarantee that no untrusted surface can ever inject a value that bypassed it.
- **Effect:** No table, column, constraint, RPC, migration, or code file is created or changed by this decision entry itself — it corrects the contract that migration `0042` (still not created) and the still-unwritten pipeline/reprocessing code must implement. Replaces the previously proposed SQL/TypeScript cross-implementation fixture test (never implemented) with three tests, none yet written: (1) TypeScript-side regression fixtures run directly against `lib/evidence/normalize-entity-text.ts`, covering the accent, Unicode, case-folding edge case, punctuation/symbol, legal-suffix, whitespace, and domain fixtures already enumerated in the feasibility audit; (2) tests confirming `analyze-probe-response.ts` and `scripts/backfill-entity-observations.ts` both import and call this exact module — never a duplicated or parallel implementation; (3) a static test confirming no API route or other externally-reachable code path accepts or forwards a caller-supplied `normalizedText`/`normalized_text` value into the entity-resolution write path.
- **Revisit trigger:** If any future requirement introduces a less-trusted caller of this RPC — exposing it, even indirectly, through a public endpoint or a third-party integration — this trust hypothesis is voided, and Unicode-equivalence verification (or one of the other architecture options surfaced in the feasibility audit — PL/Python with a real Unicode library, or HMAC-based authenticity verification via `pgcrypto`) must be revisited as its own separate decision before that exposure is authorized.

## D-020 — M5 job completion is gated on the current attempt's reconciled, completed state and its two authoritative receipts; older analysis generations count toward cost but never toward the outcome

- **Date:** 2026-08-21
- **Status:** Approved
- **Approval date:** 2026-08-21
- **Decision:** Every `protocol_execution_jobs` row gains a nullable `current_attempt_id`, naming at most one `protocol_attempts` row as the tentative this job currently owns. A composite foreign key — `protocol_execution_jobs(current_attempt_id, id, project_id)` referencing `protocol_attempts(id, job_id, project_id)` — makes it structurally impossible to point at an attempt belonging to a different job, even one in the same project; a bare `(current_attempt_id, project_id)` FK would not have prevented that. `current_attempt_id` is null before the first admission; `admit_and_reserve_attempt_for_job` sets it, unconditionally and in the same statement as its existing status transition, to the attempt it just created — no later, separate write that a subsequent failure could leave half-applied. `authorize_operator_retry`'s `retry_full_attempt` branch replaces it atomically with the new attempt's id, in the same statement as its own status transition; `retry_analysis_only` never touches it, since the current attempt does not change when only a new analysis generation is reserved under it. `complete_protocol_execution_job` is rewritten, in this exact order, to never again return `SUCCEEDED` without evidence: lock the job by `(id, project_id)` → absent is `JOB_NOT_FOUND`; already `succeeded` is `ALREADY_SUCCEEDED` with no mutation, checked before any fencing test, because a terminal job has no live owner left to validate; any other terminal status (`failed`, `needs_review`, `abandoned_exhausted`) is `CONFLICT` with no mutation; only then is ownership checked (`claim_token`/lease/status), returning `JOB_NOT_OWNED` on mismatch; a null `current_attempt_id` is `NO_CURRENT_ATTEMPT`; the current attempt is locked by the exact triple `(id, job_id, project_id)`, never by attempt id alone, with a defensive `CURRENT_ATTEMPT_NOT_FOUND` in case the foreign key were ever bypassed; an attempt whose `terminalized_at` is still null is `ATTEMPT_NOT_RECONCILED`; an attempt reconciled to anything other than `completed` is `ATTEMPT_NOT_COMPLETED`; every `protocol_external_calls` row of that attempt is then locked in the deterministic order `(call_kind, generation)` — the same order for every caller, never row-arrival order — and any row still `reserved`/`in_progress` is `EXTERNAL_CALLS_NOT_TERMINAL`; the Perplexity generation-1 call must be `completed` and carry a matching `provider_call_receipts` row, or the result is `PERPLEXITY_NOT_COMPLETED`; the analysis generation with the highest `generation` number for that attempt — and only that one — must be `completed` and carry a matching `analysis_call_receipts` row, or the result is `ANALYSIS_NOT_COMPLETED`. Every earlier analysis generation (`failed`, `timed_out`, `cancelled`, or `completed`) remains exactly as append-only as before and is still summed by `reconcile_attempt_for_job` into the attempt's reconciled cost; none of them is read by the completion gate to decide the outcome — an old success cannot make the job succeed if the newest generation is still active or has failed, and an old failure cannot block success once the newest generation has completed. No step anywhere in this gate requires `actual_cost_usd` to be non-null: an honestly reconciled unknown cost (already permitted by the existing reconciliation rule) is accepted exactly as-is. Only after every one of these checks passes does the job's `status` become `succeeded`; `claim_token`/`lease_expires_at` are left untouched on success, unchanged from the already-documented audit-trail behavior. Two bugs found by real local execution against a disposable Postgres instance in the same milestone are fixed in the same file as a precondition for this decision, not as part of it: the two `_admit_and_reserve_attempt_core`/`_reserve_additional_analysis_generation_core` call sites that supplied a redundant column-definition list to an already-named-`RETURNS TABLE` function (a hard Postgres error on every real invocation, invisible to static text review) now use the bare `AS core` alias; and `claim_protocol_execution_job`'s reclaim and exhaustion branches, which previously only ever matched `status = 'running'`, now match `status in ('claimed', 'running')`, so a worker that crashes after claiming but before its first admission is reclaimed or, once `attempt_count` reaches `max_attempts`, actually abandoned — instead of remaining silently stuck in `claimed` forever.
- **Reason:** A read-only audit of the drafted M5 worker schema found that `complete_protocol_execution_job` checked only job ownership — never the attempt, never either external call, never either receipt — so a job could reach `succeeded` with zero provider or analysis evidence ever recorded, contradicting the evidence-immutability and fail-closed invariants this repository already holds itself to elsewhere. The audit also found no canonical decision anywhere in this file defining a completion predicate, an authoritative analysis generation, or an idempotent vocabulary for repeated completion calls — this entry is that missing decision, not an inference from a prior one. The "most recent generation is authoritative, earlier ones count only toward cost" rule mirrors the already-approved pattern for attempts themselves (`retry_of_attempt_id` chains, where the newest attempt is the one that matters going forward while every earlier one remains a permanent, readable ledger row) rather than inventing a new precedent. Reusing `reconcile_attempt_for_job`'s own terminal/cost computation as the single source of truth for job-level completion — instead of duplicating gating logic at the job level — keeps the two functions from silently drifting apart. The `ALREADY_SUCCEEDED`/`CONFLICT` split reuses the exact precedent already established by `reconcile_attempt_for_job`'s own `ALREADY_RECONCILED`/`CONFLICT` split, rather than overloading the pre-existing `JOB_NOT_OWNED` status to mean two unrelated things (no live owner to check, versus a real fencing mismatch).
- **Effect:** `supabase/migrations/0050_m5_protocol_execution_jobs_and_external_call_ledger.sql` gains `protocol_execution_jobs.current_attempt_id`, the supporting `protocol_attempts_id_job_project_key` unique constraint, the `protocol_execution_jobs_current_attempt_fk` composite foreign key, and a supporting index; `admit_and_reserve_attempt_for_job` and `authorize_operator_retry` are updated to maintain the pointer; `complete_protocol_execution_job`'s body is replaced as described above, with its signature, `RETURNS TABLE`, `SECURITY DEFINER`, `search_path`, owner, and every `REVOKE`/`GRANT` left unchanged. `fail_protocol_execution_job` is not modified by this decision. `scripts/test-m5-0050-schema-contract.ts` gains static assertions for the new column, constraint, pointer-maintenance call sites, and the full ordered gate. `scripts/test-m5-0050-runtime-contract.ts` is added as a real, opt-in, disposable-Postgres reproduction of the gate's truth table (including the two runtime fixes above), never run against staging or Production and never requiring a remote credential. This migration remains uncommitted and unapplied to any database as of this decision.
- **Revisit trigger:** If a future milestone introduces a second live provider surface, this decision's "exactly one Perplexity generation-1 call" precondition must be revisited alongside D-004. If a future milestone allows more than one concurrent attempt per job, the meaning of `current_attempt_id` and this gate's single-attempt assumption must be revisited as its own decision before that change is authorized.

## D-021 — M5 worker resumption never creates a second admission from a stale idempotency key; operator retries are fingerprinted and structurally separated from bounded automatic retries; `probe_run` creation is atomically fenced and replay-safe; ambiguity after a lease loss transitions to `needs_review` through one dedicated RPC; rollout never passes through an intermediate broken state

- **Date:** 2026-08-21
- **Status:** Approved
- **Approval date:** 2026-08-21
- **Decision:** `admit_and_reserve_attempt_for_job` creates a new attempt only when `protocol_execution_jobs.current_attempt_id IS NULL`, using a stable, job-scoped idempotency key (`${job.id}:initial-attempt`) that never depends on the worker, the claim token, or `attempt_count` — `attempt_count` remains strictly a claim/execution counter, never a paid-attempt identity. Whenever `current_attempt_id` is already set, regardless of the pointed attempt's status, this RPC creates nothing and returns `RESUME_CURRENT_ATTEMPT` with the full existing context (the current attempt, its Perplexity generation-1 call, and its highest-generation analysis call). Two automatic, worker-callable, structurally separate retry RPCs replace any notion of a single generic "full retry" path: `retry_full_attempt_for_job` is eligible only when the job's own current attempt is terminal-but-not-completed, no external call on it is ambiguous, and Perplexity generation 1 itself is `failed`/`timed_out`/`cancelled` with a receipt — the branch that actually failed net; `retry_analysis_generation_for_job` is eligible only when Perplexity is `completed` with a receipt and the current, authoritative (highest-generation) analysis call is `failed`/`timed_out`/`cancelled` with a receipt — never an older, non-authoritative generation. Both are replay-safe through new structural identity: `protocol_attempts.retry_of_attempt_id` (existing since migration 0047) is now protected by a composite foreign key to `protocol_attempts(id, protocol_id, project_id)` and a partial unique index, guaranteeing a retry child shares its parent's protocol and project and that at most one direct child exists per source; `protocol_external_calls` gains an analogous `retry_of_external_call_id`, a matching composite foreign key to `protocol_external_calls(id, protocol_attempt_id, project_id)`, and its own partial unique index. A replay with matching reserved costs against an already-retried source returns `ALREADY_RETRIED` with the existing child; a call with different costs against an already-retried source returns `SOURCE_ALREADY_RETRIED`, zero mutation — never a second, competing child. Neither automatic RPC ever fires when any external call on the attempt is ambiguous (`in_progress`, `request_sent_at` set, no matching receipt in either receipt table): that state is resolved exclusively by a new RPC, `mark_protocol_execution_job_needs_review_for_ambiguity`, which proves the ambiguity under the job's own lock before transitioning `status` to `needs_review` and never mutates the call, the attempt, the `probe_run`, or any evidence — it is idempotent (`ALREADY_NEEDS_REVIEW` on replay, checked before fencing since a `needs_review` job has no live owner to validate) and refuses (`NO_AMBIGUOUS_CALL`) if no such proof exists, so it can never be used to move a healthy job into review. Only `authorize_operator_retry` may resolve an ambiguous job, and it is itself corrected to be genuinely idempotent: it gains a mandatory, caller-supplied `p_idempotency_key`, replacing its previous internal `'operator-retry-' || gen_random_uuid()::text`, which generated a fresh key on every call and could log an already-approved retry as a fabricated `denied` action once a cap was exhausted by the first successful reservation — that defect is corrected, not merely documented. Every `protocol_operator_actions` row now also carries a `request_fingerprint`, a canonical `jsonb_build_object` of the request's immutable business fields (`job_id`, `project_id`, `action`, `source_attempt_id`, `source_external_call_id`, `reserved_cost_usd`, `perplexity_reserved_cost_usd`) serialized to text — never a raw ambiguous concatenation, and never including `actor`/`reason`, which remain provenance metadata outside the fingerprint. `UNIQUE (project_id, job_id, idempotency_key)` identifies one row; the fingerprint, compared after that row is located under the job's lock, decides the outcome: same key and same fingerprint returns the original result verbatim (approved or denied, without ever re-evaluating caps); same key with a different fingerprint returns `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_CONFLICT`, zero mutation — structurally impossible for a `retry_analysis_only` request to receive a `retry_full_attempt` request's approval, since `action` is itself part of the fingerprint. No exception-based "catch a `unique_violation` and quietly return the winner" mechanism exists anywhere in this decision: the job lock already serializes concurrent calls against the same job, an explicit existence check before any creation is the second defense, and the unique constraints are a last-resort signal only — any `unique_violation` that still occurs despite both is left to propagate uncaught, rolling back the entire transaction rather than risk returning a result that does not correspond to what was actually committed. `probe_run` creation and attempt-start are combined into one new RPC, `create_probe_run_and_start_attempt_for_job`, executed in a single transaction and made replay-safe by the pre-existing `protocol_attempts.probe_run_id` (migration 0047), now protected by a new composite foreign key to `probe_runs(id, project_id)`: a reserved attempt with no run creates and attaches one (`STARTED`); an already-`in_progress` attempt with a consistent run returns `ALREADY_STARTED` with that same run, creating nothing; an inconsistent link is `CONFLICT`; a terminal attempt is `ATTEMPT_TERMINAL` — no second `probe_run` is ever possible after a lost response or a reclaim. A new fenced, read-only RPC, `get_protocol_execution_job_context`, returns job, protocol, current attempt, external calls, both receipt tables, and the `probe_run` as one coherent snapshot under one lock read — the recommended way for a worker to recover state after a claim or reclaim, in preference to composing several independent `SELECT`s whose results could otherwise be read at different, inconsistent instants. A new internal-only view, `protocol_execution_job_monitoring_v1`, declared `security_invoker = true` and granted `SELECT` to `service_role` only, exposes raw counts — queued jobs and oldest-queued age, claimed/running jobs, expired leases, jobs by terminal status, attempts per job, external calls by status and kind, calls sent without a receipt, reserved/estimated/measured/unknown-cost totals, and provider/analysis latency averages sourced only from already-recorded receipt fields — with no alert threshold, since none is defined in canonical documentation. Timeouts, lease duration, and heartbeat cadence for the future worker (60s Perplexity / 30s analysis / 240s lease / 60s heartbeat, with the abandonment decision driven by the last database-confirmed `lease_expires_at` rather than a bare consecutive-failure count) are approved as explicit V1 values by this decision, since no canonical or existing application-level timeout exists on either call path today; they are not implemented in this migration, which is schema/RPC-only. Rollout never passes through an intermediate broken state: since migration `0050_m5_protocol_execution_jobs_and_external_call_ledger.sql` has never been applied to any remote environment, its three `revoke execute ... from service_role` statements (on `admit_and_reserve_attempt`, `start_attempt`, `reconcile_attempt`) are removed from it before its first remote application and deferred to a separate, later cutover migration not created by this decision — this is a relocation of *when* those revocations apply, never a reopening of *whether* they should, which D-020 already settled and which this decision does not revisit. The sequence is: apply corrected 0050 (still fully additive, M4's raw RPCs remain executable); apply this decision's migration (also additive); deploy the M5 worker, adapters, and CLI, at which point the CLI explicitly stops calling the M4 raw RPCs — not merely after their later revocation; validate in staging; only then apply the future cutover migration; only then remove the dead M4 code. No fallback exists at any point on a missing-function error, a permission error, a timeout, a network error, or a business status. The M5 protocol-execution worker and its ledger remain structurally separate from the legacy `lib/snapshot-worker/` claim/lease system; no code, table, or RPC is shared between them.
- **Reason:** A staged, read-only audit of the M5-2 worker design — conducted before any application code was written — found three concrete, evidence-backed gaps rather than hypothetical ones: `admit_and_reserve_attempt_for_job` had no guard against a second admission once `current_attempt_id` was already set, so a worker minting a fresh idempotency key after a reclaim could create an orphaned second attempt; `authorize_operator_retry`'s own internal key generation (`gen_random_uuid()` on every call) defeated the very idempotency mechanism the rest of the schema relies on, confirmed by direct inspection of its committed body, not assumed; and `_reserve_additional_analysis_generation_core`'s unconditional `max(generation) + 1` recomputation was confirmed non-idempotent by the same direct inspection. Separating `retry_full_attempt_for_job` from `retry_analysis_generation_for_job` — rather than one generic automatic retry for any terminal-non-completed attempt — avoids uselessly and incorrectly re-running Perplexity when only the analysis leg failed and a valid provider receipt already exists. Fingerprinting the operator action, rather than trusting `action` alone to disambiguate a reused key, closes a real path by which a key reused for a different intent could otherwise silently return the wrong prior decision. Direct inspection of `lib/probes/perplexity.ts` and `lib/llm/call-model.ts` found no idempotency key, client request ID, or provider-side deduplication mechanism on either call path, and no application-level timeout on either — confirming that no safe automatic retry can ever exist for the "sent, no receipt" state, and that lease/heartbeat values cannot be derived from an existing constant and must be explicitly decided rather than deferred indefinitely, which would otherwise block the worker implementation this decision exists to unblock.
- **Effect:** `supabase/migrations/0050_m5_protocol_execution_jobs_and_external_call_ledger.sql` has its three `revoke execute ... from service_role` statements removed (SHA-256 changes accordingly; `scripts/test-m5-0050-schema-contract.ts` and `scripts/test-m5-0050-runtime-contract.ts` are updated to expect the M4 raw RPCs to remain executable by `service_role` after 0050+this migration, pending the still-uncreated cutover migration). `supabase/migrations/0051_m5_protocol_worker_resumption_and_retry_safety.sql` is added: the `needs_review_reason`/`status` coherence constraint on `protocol_execution_jobs`; `idempotency_key`/`request_fingerprint` and their constraints on `protocol_operator_actions`; the corrected `_reserve_additional_analysis_generation_core` (fourth parameter) and `authorize_operator_retry` (new parameters, old signature dropped); the `retry_of_attempt_id`/`retry_of_external_call_id` composite foreign keys, unique constraints, and partial unique indexes; `retry_full_attempt_for_job`; `retry_analysis_generation_for_job`; the corrected `admit_and_reserve_attempt_for_job`; the `protocol_attempts_probe_run_project_fk` constraint and `create_probe_run_and_start_attempt_for_job`; `get_protocol_execution_job_context`; `mark_protocol_execution_job_needs_review_for_ambiguity`; and `protocol_execution_job_monitoring_v1`. Every new unique/composite constraint is preceded by a read-only preflight that raises an explicit exception on any historical collision rather than silently succeeding or backfilling. `scripts/test-m5-0051-schema-contract.ts` is added as the static contract test for this migration. No worker, adapter, CLI, or provider-client code is touched by this decision; no migration is applied to any remote environment.
- **Revisit trigger:** If a future provider integration offers a genuine idempotency key or request-scoped deduplication, the fail-closed `needs_review` rule for that specific provider must be revisited as its own decision, never loosened for all providers at once. If M5 ever allows more than one concurrent attempt per job, the single-pointer `current_attempt_id` model and the `RESUME_CURRENT_ATTEMPT` contract must be redesigned before that change is authorized. If real Perplexity/analysis latency data collected via `protocol_execution_job_monitoring_v1` shows the 60s/30s timeouts are miscalibrated, the timeout values — not the lease/heartbeat ratio — are the first parameters to revisit.

## D-022 — Amendment to D-021: idempotent replay in `authorize_operator_retry` and `retry_full_attempt_for_job` is checked before, and independently of, the state-gate the replayed call itself may have already changed

- **Date:** 2026-08-21
- **Status:** Approved
- **Approval date:** 2026-08-21
- **Decision:** Amends D-021's transactional ordering in `authorize_operator_retry` and `retry_full_attempt_for_job`, discovered by a read-only runtime diagnostic against a disposable Postgres instance, not a new capability. In `authorize_operator_retry`: after the job row is locked (`SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`), the canonical fingerprint is computed and the existing-action lookup by `(project_id, job_id, idempotency_key)` runs *before* the `status = 'needs_review'` gate, not after it. Same key and same fingerprint returns the exact recorded result (`APPROVED`/`DENIED` with its original attempt/external call), without mutation, regardless of the job's current status. Same key with a different fingerprint returns `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_CONFLICT`, zero mutation, likewise regardless of status. Only when no row exists for that key is `status <> 'needs_review'` consulted, returning `JOB_NOT_IN_NEEDS_REVIEW` for a genuinely new key against a job that has already moved on. In `retry_full_attempt_for_job`: after the job is locked and ownership/fencing is verified, the existing-child lookup by `retry_of_attempt_id` (an identifier that never changes once a retry child is created) runs *before* the `current_attempt_id` equality gate. A matching-intent existing child returns `ALREADY_RETRIED` with that same child and its calls; a different-intent existing child returns `SOURCE_ALREADY_RETRIED`, zero mutation. Only when no child exists yet is `current_attempt_id` compared against `p_source_attempt_id`, gating the creation of a genuinely new retry. Both replay lookups additionally have their two return-list scalar subquery expressions corrected from `(SELECT alias.* FROM ... WHERE ...)` to the bare composite row form `(SELECT alias FROM ... WHERE ...)` — the starred form is invalid as a single scalar expression in a `RETURN QUERY SELECT` list and raised `subquery must return only one column` on every real invocation reaching that branch. No exception-based `unique_violation` absorption is introduced anywhere by this amendment; the job lock plus these now-correctly-ordered explicit checks remain the only real defenses, exactly as D-021 already required.
- **Reason:** A single, opt-in, disposable-stack runtime execution of the RPCs D-021 specifies — followed by a strict read-only diagnostic pass, never a blind retry — found that both functions gated their idempotent-replay check behind a job-state condition that a successful call, under this exact same idempotency key, itself changes as a side effect (job status leaving `needs_review`; `current_attempt_id` moving to the new child). This meant a true concurrent resubmission of the identical request — precisely the case an idempotency key exists to protect against — observed a different, incorrect result (`JOB_NOT_IN_NEEDS_REVIEW` / `SOURCE_NOT_CURRENT_ATTEMPT`) instead of the idempotent replay, confirmed both in sequential replay and in genuine two-process concurrency (`Promise.all` over two real `psql` processes racing the same row). The scalar-subquery defect was confirmed via the exact server-side error and `CONTEXT` line reproduced against the disposable instance, not inferred from static text review alone.
- **Effect:** `supabase/migrations/0051_m5_protocol_worker_resumption_and_retry_safety.sql` is edited in place (not superseded by a new migration number, since it has never been applied to any remote environment): the ordering and scalar-subquery corrections above are applied directly to `authorize_operator_retry` and `retry_full_attempt_for_job`. `scripts/test-m5-0051-schema-contract.ts` gains static assertions for the corrected ordering and the absence of the starred-subquery pattern anywhere in the migration. `scripts/test-m5-0051-runtime-contract.ts` is corrected for the harness defects the same diagnostic found (unrelated to this amendment's SQL change) and re-run once, opt-in, on a fresh disposable stack, including the same two real-concurrency races, to confirm both replay guarantees now hold under true concurrency. D-020 is not modified by this entry.
- **Revisit trigger:** If a future RPC introduces its own idempotency-key replay check, the same ordering discipline — replay lookup before any state gate the replayed call's own success mutates — must be applied there from the start, not discovered again by runtime audit.

## D-023 — M5-2 uses atomic job execution, bounded provider calls, enqueue-only dispatch, and no M4 fallback

- **Date:** 2026-08-21
- **Status:** Approved
- **Approval date:** 2026-08-21
- **Decision:** Four pre-implementation decisions for M5-2, resolving the blockers raised by the read-only M5-2 audit, none requiring a new migration. (1) **Canonical start.** The M5 worker calls `create_probe_run_and_start_attempt_for_job` exclusively for starting an attempt against a probe run. `start_attempt_for_job` (migration 0050) remains present in the schema for backward compatibility only — it is not canonical: no corresponding method is added to `DbAdapter`, the worker never calls it, and it is not revoked before the final cutover migration (unrelated to this decision, which does not authorize any revocation). (2) **Bounded provider calls.** M5-2 must implement, at the application layer: a 60-second timeout on the Perplexity call and a 30-second timeout on the analysis call, each via `AbortController` or an equivalent mechanism that is actually covered by a test exercising the timeout path (a mocked slow/hanging call that is observed to abort, not merely a code review of the wiring); the durable `sent` marker (`mark_external_call_sent_for_job`) is written before the outbound HTTP call is issued, never after; an external call found `in_progress` with `request_sent_at` set and no receipt after a crash or reclaim is routed to `needs_review` (via `mark_protocol_execution_job_needs_review_for_ambiguity`), never silently retried — an ambiguous outcome is never resolved by guessing. (3) **Evidence and cost honesty.** For both external calls: the SHA-256 fields already defined by migrations 0050/0051 are populated from the real outbound/inbound bytes wherever such a field exists on that call kind (`provider_call_receipts.raw_response_sha256` for Perplexity; `analysis_call_receipts.request_sha256`/`response_sha256` for analysis — Perplexity's own receipt row has no raw-request-hash column today, and this decision does not add one); the provider's response request ID is captured whenever the provider actually returns one; token/request usage is captured whenever the provider actually returns it; `actual_cost_usd` is populated only when a real monetary cost is returned by the provider or computable under an explicitly approved, fixed pricing rule tied to real usage data — otherwise `actual_cost_usd` stays `NULL` and `cost_basis` is recorded as `unknown` (an addition to the `cost_basis` vocabulary, application-level only in this Stage A, since `protocol_external_calls.cost_basis` presently checks only `measured`/`estimated`; reconciling that CHECK constraint against a genuine `unknown` value at the schema level is cutover-migration territory, not this decision or Stage A). The historical flat constant (`$0.03`, `PROTOCOL_ACTUAL_COST_PER_LIVE_PROBE_USD`) must never be written to `actual_cost_usd` as if it were a real measured or provider-computed cost. No new column and no migration `0052` is introduced by this decision. (4) **CLI activation, deferred.** At the M5-2 application cutover (not Stage A), `mvp-scan --protocol-id` becomes exactly: call `enqueue_protocol_execution_job`, then exit — it never calls `runProtocolGatedBaseline` again, never dual-writes to both the M4 direct path and an M5 job for the same protocol, and never falls back to the M4 direct path if the enqueue call or the worker is unavailable; it fails closed and exits non-zero. The CLI must not import `LeaseRenewer` or anything else from `lib/snapshot-worker/` — the M5 worker's own lease (`protocol_execution_jobs.claim_token`/`lease_expires_at`, `renew_protocol_execution_job_lease`) is the only lease mechanism M5-2 code may depend on. This fourth item is explicitly out of scope for Stage A, which touches no CLI file; it is recorded now so Stage A's types/adapters are built toward the contract this activation will require, not toward a different one discovered later.
- **Reason:** The read-only M5-2 audit found three genuine blockers and one design gap, each traced to a specific file/line rather than assumed: `start_attempt_for_job` (0050) and `create_probe_run_and_start_attempt_for_job` (0051) both exist with incompatible contracts (the former requires a pre-created `probe_run_id`, reproducing the exact non-atomic gap 0051 was written to close) and nothing in the schema or documentation said which one a future caller should use; `lib/probes/perplexity.ts` and `lib/llm/call-model.ts`, read in full, confirmed no timeout, no idempotency key, no outbound request ID, and no usage/cost capture on either provider call path, while D-021 already approved 60s/30s timeout values that had no implementation path without touching these two files; `scripts/mvp-scan.ts` was found to import `LeaseRenewer`/`readLeaseRenewalConfigFromEnv` from `lib/snapshot-worker/lease-renewal.ts` for its own `--protocol-id` path's lease bookkeeping — a real, load-bearing coupling to the legacy snapshot-worker's lease system that D-021's separation clause ("no code, table, or RPC is shared between them") would otherwise be silently violated by if the M5-2 worker inherited the same CLI shell uncritically; and no decision anywhere specified what `--protocol-id` should do once the M5 worker exists, leaving a real risk of two independent admission paths (M4 direct, M5 job-scoped) racing the same `protocol_id` with no structural guard against it.
- **Effect:** No file is changed by this decision entry itself beyond `docs/DECISIONS.md`. It authorizes M5-2 Stage A (types and adapter contracts in `lib/db/types.ts`, `lib/db/memory-adapter.ts`, `lib/db/supabase-adapter.ts`, plus `scripts/test-m5-2-adapter-contract.ts`) to proceed without exposing `start_attempt_for_job` or `reserve_additional_analysis_generation_for_job` as new canonical `DbAdapter` methods, and fixes the target contract for the later provider-timeout tranche and the later CLI-cutover tranche so each can be built once, correctly, rather than revised after the fact. `reserve_additional_analysis_generation_for_job` remains revoked from `service_role` since migration 0051 (D-021/D-022); this decision does not reopen that.
- **Revisit trigger:** If a future requirement needs `start_attempt_for_job`'s non-atomic two-step shape for a legitimate reason (e.g. a probe run created by a step genuinely separate from the attempt start), that need must be brought back as its own decision rather than quietly promoting the function to canonical. If Perplexity or the analysis provider later exposes a genuine idempotency key or request-scoped deduplication, the ambiguous-call-after-crash rule in item (2) should be revisited alongside D-021's own revisit trigger for the same condition.

## D-024 — Abandoned-job reactivation is a fingerprinted, replay-safe operator action

- **Date:** 2026-08-22
- **Status:** Approved
- **Approval date:** 2026-08-22
- **Decision:** `reactivate_abandoned_job`'s original 4-parameter signature (`p_job_id uuid, p_project_id uuid, p_actor text, p_reason text`, migration 0050) is dropped explicitly, without `CASCADE`, and replaced by a 5-parameter signature adding a mandatory, caller-supplied `p_idempotency_key text` — never a value generated inside the function. Exactly the same structural pattern D-021 already established for `authorize_operator_retry` governs this RPC: after the job is locked (`SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` on `project_id + job_id`), a canonical request fingerprint is computed via the existing `_sha256_hex` helper over a `jsonb_build_object` containing exactly three keys — `job_id`, `project_id`, and a literal `action` value of `reactivate_abandoned` — with `actor`/`reason` excluded from the fingerprint as provenance metadata, exactly as D-021 excludes them from `authorize_operator_retry`'s fingerprint. The existing-action lookup by `(project_id, job_id, idempotency_key)` — the same unique constraint `protocol_operator_actions_project_job_key_key` already enforces, unchanged, with no `action` column added to it — runs *before* the job's `abandoned_exhausted` status is ever checked, mirroring D-022's ordering discipline exactly: same key and same fingerprint returns the exact previously recorded decision (`REACTIVATED` or the job's original denial code) verbatim, with zero mutation, regardless of the job's current status; same key with a different fingerprint returns `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_CONFLICT`, zero mutation. Only when no row exists for that key does the function inspect the job: a job not `abandoned_exhausted` is refused with an explicit `JOB_NOT_ABANDONED` denial, itself logged as a `denied` `protocol_operator_actions` row (not merely returned and forgotten, so a replay of the refusal is equally idempotent); a genuinely `abandoned_exhausted` job is reactivated (`status = 'queued'`, `attempt_count = 0`, `worker_id`/`claim_token`/`lease_expires_at`/`failure_reason` cleared) and an `approved` `protocol_operator_actions` row is inserted in the same transaction as that update — never as a separate, later write. As with every other operator RPC since D-021, no `EXCEPTION WHEN unique_violation` or any other exception-absorption exists anywhere in this function; the job lock plus the explicit existing-action check above are the only real defenses, and an unexpected constraint violation is left to propagate uncaught, rolling back the entire transaction.
- **Reason:** The M5-2 Stage A audit found, by direct reading of migrations 0050 and 0051 together, that `reactivate_abandoned_job`'s own `INSERT INTO protocol_operator_actions` (unchanged since 0050) supplies neither `idempotency_key` nor `request_fingerprint`, while 0051 made both columns `NOT NULL` with no default — meaning every real call to this RPC raises a not-null violation that rolls back the job-reactivation `UPDATE` in the same breath, so the function cannot succeed at all as originally written. Beyond the immediate NOT NULL defect, the original signature had no idempotency mechanism whatsoever: a duplicate operator submission (e.g. a double-click, or a retried request after an ambiguous network response) had no structural way to be recognized as a replay rather than a second, independent decision — precisely the class of defect D-021 already corrected for `authorize_operator_retry`, and the same fix belongs here for the same reason.
- **Effect:** `supabase/migrations/0051_m5_protocol_worker_resumption_and_retry_safety.sql` gains a new section: the old 4-arg `reactivate_abandoned_job` is dropped (no `CASCADE`) and replaced by the 5-arg, fingerprinted, replay-safe version described above; `REVOKE`/`GRANT` are reissued verbatim for the new signature (`service_role`-only `EXECUTE`, as before). `supabase/migrations/0050_m5_protocol_execution_jobs_and_external_call_ledger.sql` is not modified — the original definition there is superseded by this migration's `DROP FUNCTION` + `CREATE OR REPLACE FUNCTION`, the same technique 0051 already uses for `admit_and_reserve_attempt_for_job` and `authorize_operator_retry`. `scripts/test-m5-0051-schema-contract.ts` gains static assertions for the corrected signature, ordering, and ACL. `scripts/test-m5-0051-runtime-contract.ts` gains real disposable-Postgres scenarios covering first reactivation, idempotent replay after the job's status has since changed, idempotency-key reuse conflict, denial-and-replay of a non-abandoned job, and project/job scoping. `lib/db/types.ts`/`lib/db/memory-adapter.ts`/`lib/db/supabase-adapter.ts` are updated so `ReactivateAbandonedJobInput` requires `idempotencyKey`, both adapters expose the same idempotent/fingerprinted contract, and the interim behavior that deliberately threw to surface the pre-existing NOT NULL defect (recorded in the M5-2 Stage A report) is removed now that the underlying defect is fixed. No migration `0052` is created; migration `0050` is not modified.
- **Revisit trigger:** If a future milestone needs `reactivate_abandoned_job` to also carry a source-attempt or source-external-call reference (mirroring `retry_full_attempt_for_job`/`retry_analysis_generation_for_job`'s structural replay keys), that need must be brought back as its own decision — this entry's fingerprint intentionally covers only `job_id`/`project_id`/`action`, since reactivation has no such source to reference.

## D-025 — One protocol execution job advances through every planned baseline slot sequentially

- **Date:** 2026-08-22
- **Status:** Approved
- **Approval date:** 2026-08-22
- **Decision:** `protocol_execution_jobs` gains `next_baseline_ordinal integer not null default 1` (`>= 1`, preflighted) and `protocol_attempts` gains `baseline_ordinal integer null` (`>= 1` when set, preflighted; every row that itself carries a `job_id` must carry a `baseline_ordinal` — `protocol_attempts_job_baseline_ordinal_check`, preflighted against real historical rows before being added). A partial unique index, `protocol_attempts_job_baseline_ordinal_root_key` on `(job_id, baseline_ordinal) where retry_of_attempt_id is null and job_id is not null`, guarantees at most one root attempt per slot per job — preflighted, and defense-in-depth only, since the job row's own `FOR UPDATE` lock in `admit_and_reserve_attempt_for_job` already serializes concurrent admissions for the same slot before either caller could reach it. Historical M4 rows (`job_id is null`) are untouched and keep `baseline_ordinal null` forever. **Admission** (`admit_and_reserve_attempt_for_job`): the D-021 `current_attempt_id is not null -> RESUME_CURRENT_ATTEMPT` guard is unchanged and still checked first, regardless of that attempt's status. Only when `current_attempt_id is null` does this decision's new logic run: the caller-supplied `p_idempotency_key` must equal exactly the deterministic identity `${job_id}::text || ':baseline:' || next_baseline_ordinal::text` — never a free-form or caller-chosen value — or the call returns `INVALID_IDEMPOTENCY_KEY` with zero mutation; a defensive `BASELINE_SLOTS_EXHAUSTED` guard (next_baseline_ordinal > protocols.planned_baseline_runs) precedes the core admission call and should be unreachable in normal operation, since a succeeded job is already refused by the pre-existing `JOB_NOT_OWNED` fencing check. On success, the freshly reserved root attempt is stamped with `baseline_ordinal = next_baseline_ordinal` in the same function call as its own reservation, before `current_attempt_id` is set to it. **Full retry** (`retry_full_attempt_for_job`, and `authorize_operator_retry`'s `retry_full_attempt` branch): the child attempt is stamped with the source's `baseline_ordinal` verbatim, in the same function call as its own reservation — a retry never mints a new ordinal, and `next_baseline_ordinal` is never touched by either retry path. **Analysis-only retry** (`retry_analysis_generation_for_job`) is untouched by this decision: it never creates a new `protocol_attempts` row, so no `baseline_ordinal` handling is needed there. **Completion** (`complete_protocol_execution_job`): every existing D-020 gate — job lock, `ALREADY_SUCCEEDED`/`CONFLICT` before fencing, `JOB_NOT_OWNED`, the exact current-attempt lock, `ATTEMPT_NOT_RECONCILED`/`ATTEMPT_NOT_COMPLETED`, the deterministic external-calls lock, `EXTERNAL_CALLS_NOT_TERMINAL`, `PERPLEXITY_NOT_COMPLETED`, `ANALYSIS_NOT_COMPLETED` — runs exactly as D-020 defined it, unchanged. A null `current_attempt_id` is now two distinct cases rather than one: `next_baseline_ordinal = 1` (never yet admitted) is the original `NO_CURRENT_ATTEMPT`; `next_baseline_ordinal > 1` (already advanced past a completed slot, awaiting the next admission) idempotently replays `NEXT_BASELINE_REQUIRED` with zero mutation — the same outcome the prior advancement itself returned. Once every D-020 gate passes for the current attempt, a new check decides the outcome: `baseline_ordinal < protocols.planned_baseline_runs` clears `current_attempt_id`, increments `next_baseline_ordinal` by exactly 1, and returns `NEXT_BASELINE_REQUIRED` — the job's `status` stays `running`, `claim_token`/`lease_expires_at` untouched, and `succeeded` is never returned; only once the last planned ordinal's attempt clears every gate does the job reach `succeeded`, exactly as before. **Enqueue** (`enqueue_protocol_execution_job`): `protocols.planned_baseline_runs = 0` is refused with a new `NO_BASELINE_RUNS_PLANNED` outcome, before the pre-existing `ALREADY_EXISTS` lookup and before any row is created — `protocol_execution_jobs_protocol_id_key`'s total (non-partial) `UNIQUE(protocol_id)` is untouched, so a second enqueue for the same protocol still always returns `ALREADY_EXISTS`, exactly as before; recycling a protocol's job, or advancing past its planned baseline runs into an automatic retest, remain out of scope — retest stays a separate `protocols` row (D-021's M7 note) with its own `protocol_id` and its own job. **Context and monitoring**: `get_protocol_execution_job_context` gains one new output column, `completed_baseline_slots integer`, computed as `count(distinct baseline_ordinal)` among this job's `completed` attempts — deliberately not restricted to root attempts, since a slot's completion may be reached by its root or by a retry child that inherited its ordinal, and restricting to root-only would silently undercount every slot recovered by a retry; `next_baseline_ordinal` and `planned_baseline_runs` are not duplicated here since they are already visible via the returned `job`/`protocol` rows. `protocol_execution_job_monitoring_v1` gains `jobs_awaiting_next_baseline_slot` (`status = 'running' and current_attempt_id is null`, a state that by construction only ever means "waiting for the next slot's admission") and `baseline_slots_completed_total` (`count(distinct (job_id, baseline_ordinal))` among `completed` attempts globally — never a raw `protocol_attempts` count, and never root-only, for the identical undercounting reason).
- **Reason:** A read-only audit requested before any Stage B worker work, focused specifically on whether 0050/0051 as committed (`85c9b974a6389ced0cb0cc1632d6d0c981c959e9`) could actually execute a protocol whose `planned_baseline_runs > 1` — the exact shape already used by the real, previously-validated `smoke4` staging fixture (`planned_baseline_runs = 2, planned_retest_runs = 1`) — found that it structurally could not. `admit_and_reserve_attempt_for_job`'s own D-021 comment already said so explicitly: `current_attempt_id` is resumed "quel que soit le statut de cette tentative" (regardless of that attempt's status), including a `completed` one, so a second admission call could never create a second attempt; `complete_protocol_execution_job`'s body (verified by direct reading, zero references to `protocols`, `planned_baseline_runs`, or `run_cap` anywhere in it) succeeds the job after exactly the first baseline attempt clears the D-020 gate, with total indifference to how many baseline runs were actually planned — a fail-open defect relative to this repository's own evidence-honesty invariant ("incomplete or inconsistent evidence fails closed"), not merely an unimplemented feature. D-020's and D-021's own revisit triggers already anticipated exactly this gap ("if a future milestone allows more than one concurrent attempt per job, ... must be revisited as its own decision before that change is authorized") — this entry is that decision, triggered by the audit rather than discovered later by a real multi-baseline protocol silently under-sampling in production. Every existing 0051 runtime/adapter fixture was confirmed (via direct inspection of `_m5_runtime_test_helper_make_fixture`'s hardcoded `1, 0` insert and `scripts/test-m5-2-adapter-contract.ts`'s `plannedBaselineRuns: 1`) to exercise only one baseline attempt plus its retries, never `planned_baseline_runs > 1` — confirming the gap had never actually been exercised by any test in this repository, static or runtime, before this decision. Counting completed slots by root-attempt-only was tried first during this same implementation and caught by this decision's own new adapter-contract test (a full-retry recovery scenario, D-025 test 5) undercounting a genuinely completed slot to zero when only its retry child — never its failed root — reached `completed`; switching to a distinct-ordinal count fixed it without weakening the "never double-count a retry" property, since every node in a retry chain shares its root's ordinal by construction.
- **Effect:** `supabase/migrations/0051_m5_protocol_worker_resumption_and_retry_safety.sql` gains the `next_baseline_ordinal`/`baseline_ordinal` columns, their checks, and the partial unique index (all preflighted); `admit_and_reserve_attempt_for_job`, `retry_full_attempt_for_job`, and `authorize_operator_retry` are amended in place (not superseded by a new migration number, since 0051 has never been applied to any remote environment); `complete_protocol_execution_job` and `enqueue_protocol_execution_job` (both originally defined in migration 0050) are overridden via a new `CREATE OR REPLACE FUNCTION` section appended to 0051 — the exact technique 0051 already uses for `admit_and_reserve_attempt_for_job` itself; `get_protocol_execution_job_context` and `protocol_execution_job_monitoring_v1` gain the progression columns described above. Migration `0050` is not modified; no migration `0052` is created. `lib/db/types.ts` gains `ProtocolAttempt.baselineOrdinal`, `ProtocolExecutionJob.nextBaselineOrdinal`, `GetProtocolExecutionJobContextResult.completedBaselineSlots`, and the `INVALID_IDEMPOTENCY_KEY`/`BASELINE_SLOTS_EXHAUSTED`/`NO_BASELINE_RUNS_PLANNED`/`NEXT_BASELINE_REQUIRED` outcome values; `lib/db/memory-adapter.ts` and `lib/db/supabase-adapter.ts` mirror the same contract exactly, including the distinct-ordinal counting fix. `scripts/test-m5-0051-schema-contract.ts` gains static assertions for every element above; `scripts/test-m5-0051-runtime-contract.ts` gains three new disposable-Postgres scenarios (a full two-slot `planned_baseline_runs = 2, planned_retest_runs = 1` lifecycle including the wrong-ordinal-key refusal, the idempotent post-advancement replay, and a lease-loss/reclaim between slots; a failed-root-recovered-by-retry slot counted once despite three total attempts across two slots; a `planned_baseline_runs = 0` enqueue refusal) plus a third real two-process concurrency race (two concurrent admissions for the same second slot, proving the pre-existing job-row lock still serializes a second slot's admission exactly as it already did the first); `scripts/test-m5-2-adapter-contract.ts` gains the equivalent MemoryAdapter-level scenarios. No worker, CLI, or provider-client code is touched by this decision; no migration is applied to any remote environment.
- **Revisit trigger:** If a future milestone needs an automatic retest to run as additional ordinals *within* the same baseline job rather than as a separate protocol/job (this decision explicitly keeps retest as its own `protocols` row per D-021's existing M7 note), that reversal must be brought back as its own decision. If a future requirement needs job recycling — reusing one `protocol_execution_jobs` row's `protocol_id` slot after a terminal outcome — `protocol_execution_jobs_protocol_id_key`'s total `UNIQUE(protocol_id)` constraint must be revisited explicitly, not loosened as a side effect of an unrelated change. If real worker telemetry shows `BASELINE_SLOTS_EXHAUSTED` is actually reachable in practice (not merely defensive), the condition that lets `next_baseline_ordinal` exceed `planned_baseline_runs` must be found and fixed as its own defect, not papered over by relaxing this guard.
