# Zypact V1 Pilot Runbook

- **Status:** Canonical founder-operated V1 runbook
- **Runbook date:** 2026-08-16
- **Offer:** Evidence-Backed Shortlist Diagnostic + Registered Retest
- **Engines:** Perplexity Sonar; and ChatGPT (the OpenAI Responses API with web search) once its worker switch is turned on under its own authorisation (D-053, D-061). Each is its own directional measurement; nothing is combined across them.

## 1. Purpose

This runbook defines how the first Zypact pilots are qualified, sold, operated, reviewed, and delivered while the product remains hybrid and founder-led.

It is designed to let sales and customer learning continue without sending legacy reports or promising that Zypact has already established causal lift.

## 2. Pilot boundaries

The pilot provides:

- approved buyer-question intake;
- a bounded baseline on each engine, each under its own recorded protocol;
- immutable raw evidence and traceability;
- prompt-scoped observed competitors, claims, citations, and sources;
- one evidence-backed intervention hypothesis;
- implementation and observation-window tracking;
- registered same-question retest;
- an Outcome Card reporting observed change, no clear change, or an inconclusive result.

The pilot does not provide:

- a guarantee of recommendation, citation, lead, sale, or revenue;
- what every buyer sees in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot, Claude or any other AI tool, or a score combined across engines;
- a global company visibility score;
- proof that an intervention caused a change;
- unlimited runs or open-ended monitoring;
- full-service content, PR, or development execution unless contracted separately;
- a finished self-serve platform experience during the founder-led phase.

### 2.1 Pilot Mode (D-051)

Pilot Mode is the managed pilot the founder approved on 2026-11-14. The team runs every measurement and retest from the admin console, and the customer views the results. Until the customer login shows results, they reach the customer by private link.

With ChatGPT, the ceiling is 10 USD on its first supervised day, then 20 USD, each set at its own step on both hosts (D-059 item 4). Nothing a customer can do starts paid work. The first paid pilot run waits for step 1 (D-052) to be deployed: until then a Run is not limited to its own request (`CURRENT_STATUS.md` §18.5 item 2).
- **Engines (D-053, replacing D-051's Perplexity only):** the 1,500 USD pilot includes Perplexity Sonar and ChatGPT. Each is reported as its own directional measurement of one AI engine, with no score combined across engines and no claim about other assistants. ChatGPT measures nothing for a pilot until its confirmation run passes, its setting is approved, it is wired into the worker and its deploy is authorised. Until then a pilot starts on Perplexity, and the customer's change waits until both baselines are taken. Never state an overall visibility score.
  - **2026-11-18 (D-061):** the first three conditions are met. The confirmation run passed, the setting was approved (D-050), and phase B wires ChatGPT into the worker under the same approval, admission and reservation as Perplexity, with its own caps (not yet applied or deployed). The fourth is [`PILOT_PRODUCTION_PLAN.md`](PILOT_PRODUCTION_PLAN.md) Part C: the worker switch first, then the console's ChatGPT gate.
- **Shape (D-053):** 15 approved buyer questions (20 at most), 5 answers per question on each engine, a retest of up to 5 questions on both engines, 90 days.
- **Recommended actions (D-053):** written by the team, each linked to the answers and sources it rests on, reviewed before sending. Never generated automatically, and never said to be likely to change results.
- **Offer wording:** use D-053 item 8.
- **Website ownership:** confirm that the customer controls the website before its first measurement, because automatic verification is not built. Record how it was confirmed.
- **Written approvals:** keep the customer's dated, written approval of the buyer questions and of the change to be tested, until the account records these approvals itself.
- **Logins:** one login per customer, once the login is enabled.

## 3. Initial ICP

Prioritize:

- B2B SaaS companies;
- approximately 20–200 employees;
- United States, Canada, or United Kingdom;
- English-language category and buyer questions;
- meaningful contract value or high-consideration buying process;
- identifiable competitors;
- accessible product marketing, growth, demand generation, or founder buyer;
- capacity to implement one intervention and remain through the retest window.

Deprioritize:

- low-consideration consumer/local purchases;
- companies unable to change content, product evidence, PR, or technical assets;
- prospects seeking guaranteed rankings or immediate revenue attribution;
- prospects unwilling to supply/approve buyer questions or wait for observation/retest;
- accounts that require enterprise security or procurement features outside V1.

## 4. Acquisition paths

### Request Demo

The website and outreach primary CTA is **Request Demo**.

The demo invitation should be prospect-specific and based on verified public facts. Do not attach a legacy report or generate a generic AI-written message that claims knowledge not established by research.

### One-question category benchmark

The scalable free asset is a documented benchmark for one exact category question. It may be shared with relevant prospects only after methodology and evidence review pass.

The benchmark is not a personalized company audit and must display its question, surface, dates, sample, uncertainty, and limitations.

### Manual evidence preview

For the first few design prospects, the founder may present a manually verified evidence preview during a call. It must be labeled as a recorded observation, not a stable company score.

## 5. Lead qualification call

Confirm:

1. company category and target buyer;
2. why the prospect cares now;
3. whether AI-assisted comparison appears in buyer conversations;
4. access to 10–15 possible buyer questions or a smaller defensible starting set;
5. the five highest-value questions or initial priority question;
6. ability to approve and implement one intervention;
7. decision owner and implementation owner;
8. tolerance for observation and retest timing;
9. data/confidentiality constraints;
10. budget and contracting path;
11. willingness to receive an honest negative or inconclusive outcome;
12. optional case-study/data rights, handled explicitly rather than assumed.

A verified email address or platform identity does not prove buying intent or explain non-response.

## 6. Proposal and agreement

The written pilot agreement should define:

- exact company/category scope;
- included buyer questions;
- the engines measured, each reported separately;
- diagnostic and retest deliverables;
- sampling-planning process and hard caps;
- estimated provider/hosting costs and who bears them;
- intervention responsibilities;
- observation-window and schedule assumptions;
- what happens if indexing or implementation is delayed;
- acceptance process;
- data handling and confidentiality;
- case-study/publication permissions;
- payment schedule;
- cancellation, refund, and out-of-scope rules;
- statement that results may be positive, negative, unchanged, or inconclusive;
- the smallest change the retest can show, stated before payment: with 5 runs per phase, a change of 60 points or more ([`OUTCOME_COMPARISON_PROPOSAL.md`](OUTCOME_COMPARISON_PROPOSAL.md) §6, decision 3). A pilot whose expected change is smaller must be offered a 10-run plan; until that plan exists, such a pilot cannot meet this condition;
- no causal, citation, lead, or revenue guarantee.

Legal form, entity, tax, immigration, and payment authority must be handled through appropriate professional guidance outside this runbook.

## 7. Intake

Collect the minimum necessary information:

- company name and domain;
- category and target customer;
- approved brand/product aliases;
- known competitors and aliases;
- product claims and supporting URLs/evidence;
- forbidden or unverified claims;
- buyer questions and provenance;
- buyer stage, persona, constraints, and priority;
- intervention capabilities and likely owner;
- confidentiality/publication preferences.

Do not ingest complete calls, CRM exports, customer lists, or sensitive personal data when an approved extracted buyer question is sufficient.

## 8. Question review and approval

For each proposed question:

- remove unnecessary brand naming for unbranded discovery/comparison tests;
- confirm category and decision context;
- identify ambiguity or multiple intents;
- record provenance: for an audit the team creates, say at approval whether the customer or Zypact wrote the question (D-052);
- assign stable identity/version;
- document buyer stage/persona/constraints;
- obtain explicit approval before paid execution.

Model-generated questions are suggestions until approved.

### 8.1 Adding a pilot's questions (D-054)

A managed pilot holds all of one customer's questions in one project, on **Pilots** in the founder console. Do not create one request per question.

1. Create the pilot:
   - the company and its website;
   - how you confirmed the customer controls the website;
   - the question limit (15 by default, 20 at most);
   - the start date, from yesterday to 60 days ahead. The pilot ends 90 days later. Questions can be added before the start, but not run.

   A website with an audit in progress, or with an open pilot, cannot start one.
2. Add each question only after the customer has approved it in writing. For each one, record:
   - the exact text;
   - who supplied it, the customer or Zypact;
   - where the written approval is kept;
   - the date of that approval.

   Adding a question queues its Perplexity measurement with the same plan and caps as an audit: 5 runs, a run cap of 7, a retry cap of 2 and $2.

   **ChatGPT (D-061), once it is connected** ([`PILOT_PRODUCTION_PLAN.md`](PILOT_PRODUCTION_PLAN.md) Part C):
   - Measure a question on ChatGPT in one of three ways:
     - tick **Also measure it on ChatGPT** when adding the question; it is unticked by default;
     - press **Add ChatGPT** on the question later;
     - press **Add ChatGPT to every question without it**.
   - Each queues a ChatGPT measurement of the same approved question: 5 runs, a run cap of 7, a retry cap of 2 and $4 (D-059 item 3). Nothing runs until you press Run for it on ChatGPT.
   - While any ChatGPT measurement is queued, every start, on either engine, needs $4 of room under the daily ceiling: the ceiling check adds the largest queued cap to the last 24 hours' spend.
3. Press **Run** for one question on one engine at a time, after the paid-execution switch and the daily ceiling are set. A run that failed at the provider is retried from the pilot page, on its engine, then run again. A ChatGPT request that the flex tier refused for lack of capacity is such a failure: press Retry. Nothing is resent automatically (D-059 item 5).

   A ChatGPT Run: most answers take 30-60 seconds; a call is stopped after about 5 minutes and the measurement is then held for review (item 5). If today's spending limit has no room, Run is refused; press it again later.
4. If a question's measurement stopped and cannot go on, **Retire** it from the pilot page with a reason. That covers a measurement that was abandoned, or that failed in a way Retry does not take.
   - The question and its evidence are kept.
   - It no longer counts toward the limit, and it can never be run again.
   - You can add the same approved question again.
   - A measurement that can still be retried is not retired; retry it.
   - A pilot may have at most 5 retired questions.
   - **With two engines (D-061), retiring stops the question on both.** It is allowed once one of its measurements has stopped for good, even if the other could still go on or has finished; the other engine's figures, finished ones included, are then no longer reported or counted in any total. It is refused while either is running or has a Run pending, and while the other has a run that has not finished: a Retry you approved but have not run yet, a run whose worker stopped part-way, or a run held for review. Press Run for it on that engine and let it finish (or resolve its review), then retire. A ChatGPT measurement still queued when its question is retired stays queued: every later start still needs 4 USD of room, and the queue is never empty again.
   - **With ChatGPT off** (web gate unset), a ChatGPT measurement that is only waiting for Run, or that could still be retried, can be neither run nor retried, and does not by itself let its question be retired: Retire then refuses it (for one that could be retried or has an unfinished run, it says ChatGPT must be turned back on first).
5. If a measurement is **held for review** (for example a ChatGPT call that timed out with no answer on record), the pilot page says why, for that engine. Resolve it there (D-061) with one of the request page's two resolutions, and a note of 10 to 2000 characters:
   - **Finish without the unconfirmed run;**
   - **Close as stopped.** A measurement closed as stopped cannot be retried; retire the question if it cannot go on.

   Neither runs or spends anything, and the unconfirmed run is never counted as completed.

The pilot page counts each engine's runs as the report does: failed (a timeout or a cancelled run included) and excluded after review.

6. Close the pilot when it ends, or the customer stops.
   - A close is refused while one of its measurements is running or about to start: wait for it to finish, then close.
   - Closing keeps all evidence.
   - The database then refuses any further Run, retry or claim for the pilot, including a retest of one of its questions. It also refuses a Run for a pilot past its end date.

ChatGPT shows as "Not measured" until it is wired (D-053 item 3). A pilot's results are not yet reported or sent: the combined pilot report is the next step.
- **2026-11-18 (D-061):** until ChatGPT is connected on the web host (`ZYPACT_PILOT_CHATGPT_ENABLED`, set only after the worker's switch is on), ChatGPT shows as "Not connected", and Add, Run and Retry on ChatGPT refuse. A question with no ChatGPT measurement reads "Not measured" on ChatGPT.

### 8.2 The combined pilot report (D-055)

The pilot page's **Pilot report** section builds one report for all of the pilot's questions from the stored answers. Building spends nothing, calls no AI provider or model, and shares nothing.

1. Press **Build report** once the questions have finished. Building is refused while any measurement of the pilot is still running or waiting to run; wait until it finishes. Each build saves a new revision; earlier ones are kept.
2. Press **Read revision N** to open the revision exactly as the customer will see it, labelled as a preview. Read all of it. Then read its check result:
   - **Passed its checks**: every live question finished, and every answer's evidence is complete.
   - **Needs review**: the reasons are listed, for example a question that has not finished or has incomplete evidence. Fix what you can (finish, retry or retire the question) and build again. If the report is still right to send, share it after reading every reason; the console asks you to confirm.
   - **Blocked**: it can never be shared. Tell the engineer.
3. Share from the preview, below the revision you have just read. Only the newest revision can be shared, and a shared revision can never change. The pilot page then shows the private link; send it to the customer yourself. Nothing is emailed. The first report may be shared before any suggested action exists; actions come in a later revision (D-059 item 8).
4. Build and share again when the pilot changes, for example after the retest or once suggested actions are reviewed. **Once the new revision is shared, the customer's older link stops working** (D-059 item 9, D-060): it shows only a short notice that the link has been replaced, with nothing of the report and no pointer to the new link. Send the customer the new link each time. The pilot page marks the older revision "Replaced — its link no longer opens" and no longer shows its link. The page lists the ten newest revisions; if none of them is shared, it also lists the newest shared one below them, with its link, because that link is the one that opens.

What the report says and does not say:
- Each question's result is its own: how often the company was named in that question's readable answers, the likely range, and every failed or excluded attempt.
- One total line per engine adds up readable answers over the finished questions (D-059 item 7). It is a count of answers, not a rate or a score for the company.
- **Two engines (D-061):**
  - each engine has its own tables, total and directional sentence, and nothing is added up across engines;
  - a live question not measured on an engine is named under that engine, never counted;
  - a measurement without a readable answer says so in words, never "0 of 0".
- Each engine has a "how it was measured" section: the required directional sentence, the model, how the answers were obtained, the dates, the method versions, and the rule its sources were read under. For ChatGPT it also gives the setting version, the service tier (flex) and the web-search mode (D-061).
- A retired question shows its attempt counts only, with no rate, even for a measurement that had finished on its other engine; a question retired with two measurements says one of them could not finish and how far each got.
- **A measurement a review ended** (for example after a ChatGPT timeout, resolved with **Finish without the unconfirmed run**) reads "Ended after review", never "Finished". It shows its own figures and how many planned answers it completed, is left out of its engine's total and named there, and holds the report for review.
- An engine whose questions are all retired reads "no live question is measured on it", never "0 of 0".
- A revision built before D-061 keeps the words it was built with when opened again, so its page and its stored Markdown say the same.
- Answers still being collected are counted as such, never as failed.
- Other brands and source types are shown per question, with totals across questions labelled as such.
- ChatGPT reads "not measured in this pilot yet" until it is connected (D-053 item 3).
- **Blocked (D-061):** a report is blocked when a measurement's protocol is for another engine than its own, or when one question's measurements on the two engines are not of the same approved question.
- **Needs review (D-061):** a report needs review when the setting or the source rule differs between questions of one engine, or when a ChatGPT answer has no recorded setting.
- Suggested actions appear only once reviewed (§8.3, D-057).

### 8.3 Suggested actions (D-057)

Suggested actions are written by the team, never generated, and each rests on stored answers of this pilot (D-053 item 4).

1. In the pilot page's **Suggested actions** section, press **Write a suggested action**. Fill in every field: what we observed, the suggested action, why it might matter (as a hypothesis), what else could explain it, and what we don't know yet. A pilot may have at most 30 suggested actions (D-059 item 10). Withdrawn ones do not count toward the 30; that part is D-057's *(engineer)* rule, still proposed for founder confirmation. At the limit the button is disabled and the page says why.
2. Link the answers it rests on: choose a question, then one of its completed answers, then, if the action rests on a particular source, one of that answer's own sources. Link at least one and at most ten (D-059 item 10). You cannot type a URL. Answers of either engine can be linked; a source is checked under its own engine's rule (D-061).
3. Save. The action is a draft. Wording that predicts an effect or claims a cause is refused: "will improve", "should increase", "is expected to", "guarantee", "ensure", "caused", "led to", "due to", "because of" and the like, in any spelling. Reword it as a hypothesis ("one possibility is", "may reflect", "could come from"). This applies to "What else could explain it" too.
4. Read the action and, under each link, the stored answer it rests on (**Read the stored answer**) and the source it names. Then press **I have read it: mark reviewed** and confirm. One review, by the founder, is enough (D-059 item 12). Only reviewed actions appear in a report built after that. An edit is a new draft that needs a new review; an action can have at most 50 versions.
5. Withdraw an action that no longer holds, with a reason. **A withdrawal is final: it cannot be undone** (D-059 item 11). The action stays on record, appears in no later report, and cannot be edited; write a new one if it is needed again.
6. **After any review, edit or withdrawal, build the report again before sharing.** A revision built before the change is marked out of date, and Share refuses it (D-057).

Any action in a report holds the report for your review before sharing. A link that no longer checks out (an answer since retired, or a source no longer found in the answer) blocks the report until you edit or withdraw the action.

## 9. Protocol and budget approval

Before execution, document:

- question versions;
- surface/model/mode and known configuration;
- planned baseline/retest runs;
- staged/exploratory sampling, if used;
- controls or paraphrases, if any;
- primary metric and decision threshold;
- sample and dependency limitations;
- planned attempts and cost estimate;
- project/provider hard caps;
- execution window;
- proposed observation-window rule;
- approval actor and timestamp.

If the sample required for a useful decision exceeds the cap or customer value, stop or explicitly label the work exploratory.

## 10. Baseline execution

### Preflight

- verify approval and protocol version;
- verify environment and provider configuration;
- verify project/tenant identity;
- verify remaining budget and caps;
- verify worker/operator health;
- verify output destination and privacy;
- run a dry/simulated preflight when appropriate without paid calls or persistent client evidence.

### Execution

- press Run for the one request to measure. Once D-052 is deployed (its migration, worker image and web app), the start names that request's job and the worker runs that job or nothing; a Run is refused while another is pending or the worker is busy. Before that, confirm no other job is queued before pressing Run (`CURRENT_STATUS.md` §18.5 item 2);
- reserve before every paid attempt;
- log attempt lifecycle without secrets;
- store exact response and metadata;
- reconcile failures, timeouts, retries, and spend;
- stop at approved bounds;
- do not add convenient extra runs outside the protocol.

### When a measurement stops (D-052)

Once D-052 is deployed, the request page says where and why it stopped. Before that, the console has no retry or close: a stopped measurement keeps its website blocked, and nothing may be edited by hand.

- **The AI provider returned an error or no readable reply:** retry the failed run, with a note saying why. The retry reserves one paid run inside the measurement's caps; then press Run on the requests list. The failed run stays in the run table and in the attempt reconciliation; the rate counts readable runs only.
- **Any other cause** (an answer that arrived empty, a failed analysis, a used-up cap, an abandoned job): close the request, with a note. Its evidence is kept, nothing is sent, and the website can be audited again. Tell the customer, and agree a fresh measurement if the pilot needs one.
- **A retest** that stops has no console retry or close yet. It needs a founder decision, and the intervention's one retest (0079) may be spent.
- Never edit job or attempt rows by hand.

### Founder/operator review during hybrid phase

The founder verifies that:

- counts and evidence reconcile;
- entities and aliases are sensible;
- no brand-self competitor exists;
- prompt-scoped statements use prompt-scoped evidence;
- limitations are visible;
- the result does not overstate sample stability.

This operational review does not make founder delivery mandatory when the software integrity state is `integrity_passed`.

## 11. Diagnostic delivery

The diagnostic should include:

- exact buyer questions;
- surface/configuration and execution dates;
- planned/completed/failed/excluded counts;
- observed brand presence/position per run or approved summary;
- uncertainty/sample status;
- prompt-scoped competitors and citations;
- Decision Evidence Map;
- evidence gaps and alternative explanations;
- one proposed intervention hypothesis;
- evidence still needed;
- explicit surface and causal limitations.

The diagnostic must not say the prospect lost a sale, is invisible to all AI buyers, or will improve after the recommended intervention.

## 12. Intervention registration

The founder and customer choose one intervention, then record:

- hypothesis;
- evidence supporting it;
- expected mechanism;
- action type, target, and owner;
- required approved claims/evidence;
- artifact/action hash or stable reference;
- implementation deadline;
- expected time before retrieval/retest;
- alternative explanations;
- confounders to monitor;
- outcome threshold that would change the next business decision.

Interventions may be implemented by the customer, Zypact under a separate scope, or an approved execution partner. Zypact's default role is measurement and learning, not guaranteed execution.

## 13. Implementation confirmation and observation window

Before the retest clock starts:

- confirm the intervention is actually live or completed;
- preserve implementation evidence and timestamp;
- confirm material deviations from the approved intervention;
- open the recorded observation window;
- schedule retrieval/index checks when relevant;
- record unrelated customer/market changes.

Do not retest early merely to meet a demo, sales, or reporting deadline.

## 14. Retest

The retest uses:

- the same question identity/version unless the change is explicit;
- the registered surface and configuration where possible;
- approved bounded sampling;
- treatment/control structure when justified;
- the same canonical evidence and observation rules;
- documented provider/model/configuration changes;
- visible failures and exclusions.

A disagreement or underpowered result is not forced into a positive or negative claim.

## 15. Outcome Card

Allowed status:

- `increase_observed`
- `decrease_observed`
- `no_clear_change`
- `inconclusive`

Required content:

- baseline and retest question/protocol versions;
- execution and implementation dates;
- numerator, denominator, failures/exclusions, and uncertainty;
- observed competitors, citations, claims, and evidence changes;
- controls and their behavior when used;
- confounders and protocol deviations;
- next decision;
- links to supporting evidence under access controls.

Required footer:

> **This Outcome Card documents an observed change under the recorded conditions. It does not establish that the intervention caused the change.**

## 16. Delivery states

- `integrity_passed`: generate and deliver automatically.
- `needs_review`: founder/admin resolves a specific flagged issue before delivery.
- `blocked`: do not deliver until the unsafe condition is resolved.

Every review or override is recorded. Founder review is not used to conceal or manually rewrite deterministic facts.

## 17. Existing-report remediation

Legacy reports are frozen as sales assets.

Create a private recipient inventory containing:

- prospect/company;
- date sent;
- exact artifact/version;
- sample and surface;
- figures and conclusions shown;
- known inconsistency or overstatement;
- whether the recipient engaged;
- recommended action.

Classify each case:

- **Lower risk:** raw evidence and limitations were clear; no stable or causal claim.
- **Moderate risk:** small-sample percentage or broad score was presented.
- **High risk:** inconsistent figures, brand-self competitor, unsupported cause, or false file/output contract.

Do not send a mass apology. Prepare a prospect-specific correction only when the actual artifact and relationship justify it. Silence alone does not establish that the report caused non-response.

## 18. Pilot review

After every pilot, record:

- what the customer paid for and valued;
- qualification and objection patterns;
- time to approved question and first useful evidence;
- provider and infrastructure cost;
- founder/manual hours;
- integrity/review/block incidents;
- intervention implementation delay;
- retest completion and outcome;
- whether the customer continued, referred, or stopped;
- what should be automated, standardized, or removed.

## 19. Escalation rules

Stop delivery and escalate when:

- evidence or hashes are missing;
- counts disagree;
- question/protocol identity is unclear;
- alias resolution materially changes the result;
- a report implies causality or revenue impact;
- the sample cannot support the language shown;
- provider configuration changed materially;
- client evidence may expose confidential information;
- implementation cannot be verified;
- a contractual/legal/data-permission issue is unresolved.
