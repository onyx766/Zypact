# Zypact Roadmap

- **Status:** Canonical sequencing and kill-gate roadmap
- **Roadmap date:** 2026-08-16
- **Rule:** Dates describe product planning, not contractor commitments unless included in an accepted milestone agreement
- **Direction update (D-040, 2026-11-01):** self-service is the product target.
  - **The sequence is reordered, not removed.** A self-service release with no spend may proceed ahead of phases 5 and 7. Paid self-service and automated research exposure keep their evidence gates. Every kill and reframe gate below is unchanged.
  - **The dependency-ordered steps** are in [`SELF_SERVICE_PRODUCT.md`](SELF_SERVICE_PRODUCT.md) §10. Each step needs its own authorisation, and D-040's transition rule applies.

## 1. Operating lanes

Zypact must run three separate lanes without treating one lane's progress as proof that another is complete.

| Lane | Objective | Current owner | Primary output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Methodology | Determine what can be measured and claimed | Founder with technical/statistical review | Protocol, feasibility evidence, limitations |
| Product/engineering | Build the trustworthy V1 workflow | Selected engineer plus founder acceptance | Evidence, worker, workspace, retest, reports |
| Sales/pilots | Validate buyer, urgency, payment, implementation, and retention | Founder; limited sales support | Qualified calls and paid registered-retest pilots |

## 2. Phase 0 — Documentation reset

### Target window

2026-08-16 through 2026-08-18 for drafting; merge timing depends on review and repository access.

### Deliverables

- canonical decision register;
- V1 methodology;
- V1 scope;
- engineering plan and acceptance criteria;
- pilot runbook;
- current-status inventory;
- roadmap and authority index;

### Exit gate

- active documents use one product definition and terminology;
- fixed three-run client measurement is removed;
- causal/revenue guarantees are removed;
- Perplexity is labeled as a directional surface;
- automatic delivery, review, and blocked states agree;

## 3. Phase 1 — Engineer re-estimation and selection

### Start

Only after the canonical documentation package is merged.

### Sequence

1. Send identical documentation and repository access to each serious candidate.
2. Require M0 read-only audit and explicit scope mapping.
3. Receive revised milestone tables, hours, fixed prices, dependencies, exclusions, operating costs, warranty, and schedule.
4. Compare technical understanding, evidence discipline, tests, communication, and price.
5. Choose one engineer for the first bounded milestone.
6. Sign/accept the contract and complete least-privilege onboarding.

### No false date

No trust-layer completion date is scheduled until candidate selection and contract acceptance.

### Exit gate

- one selected engineer;
- approved first milestone;
- repository-specific acceptance tests;
- staging and access plan;
- no ambiguity about sampling, uncertainty, delivery, worker, retest, or warranty.

## 4. Phase 2 — Trust layer

### Sequence

1. Security/dependency baseline.
2. Immutable exact evidence and hashes.
3. Canonical observations and entity resolution.
4. One source of truth across UI/reports/export.
5. Report integrity gates.

### Exit gate

For the same safe fixture:

- raw evidence is immutable and traceable;
- aliases and brand-self exclusion pass;
- database, dashboard, short report, full report, and export agree;
- sample and limitations are explicit;
- safe/review/blocked reports route correctly;
- no unsupported causal or broad-surface statement is deliverable.

## 5. Phase 3 — Bounded automated V1

### Sequence

1. Question/protocol approval and versioning.
2. Configurable sampling plus hard caps.
3. Admission control and reservation before paid calls.
4. Durable worker execution, retries, recovery, and monitoring.
5. Pilot workspace and evidence inspection.
6. Registered intervention and observation-window scheduler.
7. Same-question retest and uncertainty-aware Outcome Card.
8. Automatic/review/blocked delivery and operational handoff.

### Exit gate

One external staging pilot completes the full workflow without a developer editing code or database rows, and all acceptance evidence in `ENGINEERING_PLAN.md` passes.

## 6. Phase 4 — Method feasibility track

### Proposed operating window

Start planning after methodology approval. Target internal and initial replication report by 2026-10-31, subject to intervention and indexing/observation timing.

### Stage A — Internal operational feasibility

- select a non-confidential Zypact-controlled context;
- register questions, surface, sample plan, intervention, observation window, and retest before execution;
- record variance, cost, timing, failures, and confounders;
- determine whether a meaningful movement is detectable under the chosen conditions.

This produces evidence about one internal context only.

### Stage B — External replication

- repeat the method in at least two pilot contexts;
- preserve negative and inconclusive outcomes;
- compare operational cost, variance, detectability, and customer usefulness;
- obtain qualified methodological review before strengthening product claims.

### Kill/reframe gate

If meaningful changes cannot be distinguished from noise at a commercially viable cost and timeline:

- stop promising verified lift;
- retain reliable directional observation and category benchmarks if customers value them;
- do not hide failure by adding dashboards, providers, or generated assets.

## 7. Phase 5 — Sales and paid-pilot validation

### Starts now

Prospect research, personalized outreach, qualification calls, and pilot conversations may continue before automation is complete.

### Temporary restrictions

- do not attach legacy reports;
- do not sell a global company score;
- do not promise causal lift or quick retest completion;
- explain the founder-operated/manual elements;

### Initial validation targets

- three paid design partners;
- real buyer questions and intervention capacity;
- customers willing to wait through retest;
- at least two completed external replication contexts;
- evidence of value even when the outcome is negative or inconclusive;
- measured founder time and provider/infrastructure cost.

### Kill/reframe gate

Reassess the offer if qualified customers will not pay, implement, wait, or make a next decision from the output.

## 8. Phase 6 — Category benchmark acquisition

### First benchmark

- choose one narrow category aligned with the ICP;
- select one defensible buyer question;
- register and publish the method before interpreting results;
- run one bounded sample that supports the stated descriptive output;
- publish brand inclusion frequency, uncertainty, raw-evidence method, dates, and limits;
- reuse the benchmark in prospect conversations within that category.

### Exit gate

The benchmark:

- passes methodology and integrity review;
- does not become a company-wide score;
- creates qualified conversations at a cost lower than producing separate free company scans;
- can be updated on a sustainable cadence.

## 9. Phase 7 — Standardized hybrid product

### Trigger

Begin only after paid pilot and operational evidence show a repeatable workflow.

### Focus

- question portfolio;
- protocol templates derived from evidence;
- persistent Experiment Ledger;
- partner/customer implementation tracking;
- category benchmarks;
- reduced founder manual time;
- operating-cost and gross-margin visibility.

### Exclusions

Do not add broad model coverage, CRM integrations, autonomous publishing, predictive lift, 3D dashboards, or enterprise features merely to appear complete.

## 10. Phase 8 — Conditional 12–36 month evolution

If the method, customers, and economics validate:

- multi-surface adapters with surface-specific validity;
- treatment/control planning tools;
- privacy-safe CRM/call question intake;
- partner verification workflows;
- normalized Intervention–Outcome Graph;
- expected-intervention ranking only after calibration;
- verification API and benchmark products;
- enterprise governance only when customer pull justifies it.

These future phases do not authorize work.

## 11. Roadmap metrics

### Method

- registered protocols;
- baseline variance and sample sufficiency;
- detectable/underpowered tests;
- positive, negative, unchanged, and inconclusive outcomes;
- time from intervention to retrieval/retest;
- replication contexts.

### Product/operations

- completed versus failed attempts;
- duplicate-charge incidents;
- cost per completed protocol;
- founder/manual hours per pilot;
- integrity pass/review/block rate;
- recovery incidents;
- dashboard/report reconciliation defects.

### Commercial

- qualified calls;
- paid diagnostics/pilots;
- implementation completion;
- retest completion;
- continuation/referral;
- objection and non-purchase reasons;
- benchmark-sourced opportunities.

## 12. Company-level kill gates

Do not progress to broader automation, fundraising, or team expansion merely because a calendar date arrives.

Reassess when:

- measurement remains unreliable;
- the required sample is commercially uneconomic;
- customers do not pay or implement;
- retest timing destroys usefulness;
- the founder-operated workflow cannot be standardized;
- data permissions or provider access are unsafe;
- a supposed moat does not improve decisions or retention;
- a larger platform eliminates the independent-verification value.

The acceptable outcome of a kill gate is a narrower directional/benchmark product, a profitable service model, or stopping the thesis. More features are not the default response to failed evidence.
