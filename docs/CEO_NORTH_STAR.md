# Zypact CEO North Star

- **Status:** Non-binding strategic direction
- **Date:** 2026-08-16
- **Owner:** Founder/CEO
- **Authority limit:** This document cannot expand V1 scope, authorize engineering work, change methodology, or replace a signed milestone

## 1. Strategic thesis

Zypact should not compete as another broad AI visibility tracker. Its strongest possible position is a neutral **AI Shortlist Intelligence** layer for high-consideration B2B buying:

> Start with real, approved buyer questions; record what a defined AI surface returns; identify evidence-backed shortlist patterns; register one intervention; retest under documented conditions; and preserve what was learned—including negative and inconclusive outcomes.

The current V1 is deliberately narrower than that long-term ambition. It is an **Evidence-Backed Shortlist Diagnostic + Registered Retest** on Perplexity Sonar as a directional proxy.

## 2. What Zypact is trying to become

If the method, customers, and economics validate, Zypact may evolve through four layers:

1. **Trustworthy diagnostic and retest** — exact evidence, bounded protocols, one intervention, honest Outcome Card.
2. **Experiment operating system** — portfolios of buyer questions, implementation records, observation windows, controls, and repeatable operations.
3. **Independent verification and benchmarks** — surface-specific adapters, category benchmarks, partner workflows, and neutral verification across execution providers.
4. **Decision intelligence** — calibrated intervention priors and buyer-journey insight only after sufficient comparable outcomes exist.

Later layers are hypotheses, not promised roadmap items.

## 3. The company-level unit of value

Zypact does not sell a vanity score. Its unit of value is a decision-useful learning cycle:

> Approved buyer question → bounded baseline → evidence map → registered intervention → observation window → retest → auditable next decision.

A valid cycle may conclude that an increase or decrease was observed, that no clear change was observed, or that the result is inconclusive. The company earns trust by preserving all four possibilities.

## 4. Initial customer and use case

The first design partners should be English-language B2B SaaS companies with:

- meaningful deal value or buying consideration;
- a clear category and competitor frame;
- access to real or defensible buyer questions;
- a marketing/product owner able to implement one intervention;
- willingness to accept an observation window and an inconclusive result.

The first offer is hybrid and founder-operated. Human operation is acceptable when disclosed and measured; hidden manual work and invented automation are not.

## 5. What the CEO owns now

The founder/CEO owns:

- customer and buyer truth;
- category narrative and claim discipline;
- first qualification, sales, onboarding, and outcome conversations;
- question and intervention quality;
- methodology credibility and public limitations;
- pilot economics and founder-time measurement;
- engineer selection and acceptance decisions;
- legal, incorporation, contracting, and payment decisions with qualified counsel;
- the decision to narrow, continue, or stop after evidence.

The CEO should not try to become the permanent operator of every probe, migration, report, and follow-up. Manual work is used to learn which operations deserve automation.

## 6. North-star measures

### Trust and method

- percentage of paid attempts traceable to immutable evidence;
- integrity pass/review/block rate;
- protocol completion and retest completion;
- denominator reconciliation defects;
- inconclusive and failure cases preserved rather than hidden;
- replication across external pilot contexts.

### Customer value

- time to an approved decision-useful question;
- percentage of customers that implement the registered intervention;
- percentage of completed cycles that change a real customer decision;
- continuation, referral, and repeat-cycle behavior;
- value reported even when movement is negative or inconclusive.

### Economics and operations

- provider/infrastructure cost per completed protocol;
- founder/manual hours per pilot;
- gross-margin path as operations standardize;
- acquisition source, qualified-call rate, and paid-pilot conversion;
- cycle time from approval to baseline and from implementation to eligible retest.

No single “AI visibility score” is the company north star.

## 7. Data advantage hypothesis

The long-term data asset is not a warehouse of AI answers. It is a permissioned, normalized **Intervention–Outcome Graph** linking:

- buyer question, persona, stage, category, and provenance;
- surface, model/mode, protocol, and baseline variance;
- brands, competitors, claims, and sources observed;
- intervention hypothesis, implementation record, and observation timing;
- retest evidence, uncertainty, confounders, and outcome;
- customer business context, kept separate from causal claims.

This becomes an advantage only if records are comparable, consented, secure, and include failures. Volume without integrity is not a moat.

## 8. Expansion gates

### Add a provider only when

- the tested interface has documented surface validity;
- provenance and exact evidence can be preserved;
- customers value that surface;
- cost and operational reliability are acceptable;
- reports keep surface-specific results separate.

### Add integrations only when

- multiple paying customers request the same workflow;
- minimum-data extraction and privacy controls are defined;
- the integration reduces measured manual work or improves question relevance;
- it does not make one CRM or execution vendor the measurement authority.

### Add predictive ranking only when

- enough comparable interventions exist;
- calibration is measured against a naive baseline;
- uncertainty and failure cases are visible;
- the output is a bounded estimate, not a guarantee.

### Add enterprise scope only when

- customer pull justifies security, governance, SSO, and procurement work;
- the core protocol is already repeatable;
- enterprise demands do not derail the initial B2B SaaS wedge.

## 9. Fundraising readiness gates

Fundraising should finance repeatable learning and scaling, not discover whether the problem exists. A stronger readiness case includes:

- paying pilot customers;
- completed baseline/intervention/retest cycles;
- positive, negative, unchanged, and inconclusive cases handled credibly;
- repeat or referral behavior;
- evidence that manual operations can be standardized;
- a trustworthy technical owner;
- a clean data and security foundation;
- a narrow category where Zypact is meaningfully different.

Calendar pressure alone is not a fundraising reason.

## 10. Company kill and reframe gates

Reassess or narrow the thesis when:

- qualified buyers will not pay, implement, or wait for retest;
- meaningful movement cannot be distinguished from noise at viable cost;
- provider access cannot support auditable measurement;
- customers value a one-time diagnostic but not repeated cycles;
- manual delivery cannot become operationally repeatable;
- category benchmarks are more valuable than intervention retests;
- a service model is profitable but the platform/data thesis is not compounding.

The acceptable outcome may be a narrower benchmark product, a managed intelligence service, a verification tool, or stopping. More features are not the default response to failed evidence.

## 11. Permanent strategic refusals

Zypact should refuse to compete on:

- the largest prompt database;
- the most model logos;
- the cheapest tracking dashboard;
- generic content volume;
- unsupported causality or revenue attribution;
- a black-box score presented as buyer truth;
- enterprise breadth before customer pull;
- fundraising theater without method and customer evidence.

The durable brand promise is methodological honesty: show exactly what was observed, what remains uncertain, and what decision the evidence supports next.
